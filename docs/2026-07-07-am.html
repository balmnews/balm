Lake Powell, the second-largest reservoir in the United States and a critical water source for millions of people across the American West, is on course to reach unprecedentedly low levels in 2026, according to scientists and water management experts. An unusually poor snowpack this past winter failed to replenish the reservoir's levels as it historically has, accelerating a decline that has been underway for years as the region grows warmer and drier. The reservoir sits on the Colorado River and supplies water to Arizona, Nevada, California, Utah, Colorado, New Mexico, Wyoming, and parts of Mexico under a complex system of water rights agreements. Talks among the states over how to manage reduced water supply have stalled, adding urgency to the situation. If Lake Powell drops below minimum operational levels, it could affect the ability of the Glen Canyon Dam to generate hydroelectric power and deliver water to downstream users. Experts described the situation as approaching a breaking point, with the current trajectory potentially forcing emergency management decisions. The broader context is a long-term drying trend in the Colorado River basin linked to rising temperatures.
