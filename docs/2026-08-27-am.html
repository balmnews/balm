Ratko Mladic, Convicted of Genocide During Bosnian War, Dies in Prison at 84

Ratko Mladic, the Bosnian Serb military commander convicted of genocide and crimes against humanity for atrocities committed during the 1990s Balkan wars, has died in prison at age 84 while serving a life sentence. Ratko Mladic, the former Bosnian Serb general who commanded forces responsible for the worst mass atrocities in Europe since World War II, died in prison at the age of 84. He had been serving a life sentence imposed in 2017 by the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia, following his conviction for genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity. Mladic commanded Bosnian Serb forces during the 1992–1995 wars that followed the dissolution of Yugoslavia, a conflict that killed more than 100,000 people. Among the crimes for which he was convicted was the Srebrenica massacre of 1995, in which approximately 8,000 Bosnian Muslim men and boys were killed — the single largest mass killing in Europe since the Second World War. His forces also conducted a nearly four-year siege of Sarajevo, the longest siege of a capital city in modern warfare, during which civilians were subjected to sustained shelling and sniper fire. Mladic evaded capture for over a decade before being arrested in Serbia in 2011. His death in custody closes a chapter in the international criminal justice process that followed the Balkan wars, though the legacy of the conflict remains a deeply sensitive political and historical issue in the region.
