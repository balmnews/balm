United States Loses Measles Elimination Status After 26 Years as Cases and Deaths Rise

International health officials have officially revoked the United States' measles-elimination status, citing more than 5,400 cases and 10 measles-related deaths — the first fatalities recorded since the early 1990s. Mexico has also lost elimination status. The United States has officially lost its measles-elimination status, a designation it held for 26 years, after international health authorities determined that sustained domestic transmission had resumed. More than 5,400 cases have been reported across multiple states, and 10 people have died — the first measles-related deaths recorded in the country since the early 1990s. Mexico has simultaneously lost its elimination status. Measles elimination is defined as the absence of continuous transmission for 12 months or more. Health officials say the current outbreak has spread largely among unvaccinated populations. The measles virus is among the most contagious pathogens known, capable of spreading through the air and surviving on surfaces for up to two hours after an infected person has left the area. Beyond the immediate illness — which can cause pneumonia, encephalitis, and death — the virus is also known to cause immune amnesia, effectively erasing prior immunity to other diseases and leaving recovered patients more vulnerable to secondary infections for months or years afterward. Regaining elimination status is expected to take years and will require significant increases in vaccination coverage, particularly in communities where rates have fallen. Health officials continue to recommend that eligible individuals ensure they are up to date on the MMR vaccine.
