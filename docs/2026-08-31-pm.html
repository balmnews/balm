NASA Launches Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope to Study Dark Energy and Dark Matter

NASA and SpaceX launched the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, a roughly $4 billion mission designed to capture wide-field views of the universe and advance understanding of dark energy and dark matter. NASA launched the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope aboard a SpaceX rocket, marking the space agency's most expansive observational mission to date. The telescope, which cost approximately $4 billion to develop, is designed to observe large sections of the sky simultaneously — providing panoramic views that complement the detailed but narrow imaging of the Hubble and James Webb Space Telescopes. Its primary scientific objectives include investigating the nature of dark energy, the poorly understood force believed to be accelerating the expansion of the universe, and dark matter, the unseen mass that appears to shape the structure of galaxies. Together, dark energy and dark matter are estimated to constitute roughly 95 percent of the total content of the universe, yet neither has been directly observed. The Roman Telescope's wide-field infrared surveys are expected to generate data at an unprecedented scale, enabling scientists to map the distribution of matter across cosmic history and test competing theories about the universe's fundamental composition.
