NASA Launches Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope from Cape Canaveral

NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope lifted off at 7:26 a.m. ET Sunday from Cape Canaveral, Florida, beginning a multi-year mission to map dark matter, study dark energy, and identify exoplanets beyond our solar system. NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched Sunday morning from Cape Canaveral, Florida, beginning a journey to an orbit approximately one million miles from Earth. The telescope is designed to conduct a sweeping survey of the cosmos, with mission objectives that include mapping dark matter and dark energy, and identifying more than 100,000 planets beyond our solar system. The Roman telescope is considered NASA's latest flagship observatory, following in the tradition of the Hubble and James Webb Space Telescopes. It is named after Nancy Grace Roman, a pioneering astronomer who served as NASA's first Chief of Astronomy and played a foundational role in the development of the Hubble Space Telescope. The mission carries a price tag of approximately 4.3 billion dollars and survived two rounds of proposed budget cuts before reaching launch. Scientists expect the telescope's wide field of view to produce images far broader in scope than its predecessors, allowing it to survey large sections of the sky in a single observation — a capability that could accelerate discovery across multiple areas of astrophysics. The telescope is expected to begin scientific operations after completing its journey to its orbital position.
