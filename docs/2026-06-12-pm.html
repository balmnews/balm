FISA Section 702 Surveillance Authority Expires After Congress Fails to Pass Extension

The House failed to pass a three-week extension of Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, allowing the warrantless surveillance authority to expire at midnight Friday. President Trump separately announced plans to nominate Jay Clayton as director of national intelligence. A key federal surveillance tool, Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, expired after the House failed to pass a short-term extension. The provision allows U.S. intelligence agencies to collect communications of foreign targets located outside the United States, including communications that involve Americans. The failed vote on a three-week extension means the authority has lapsed, at least temporarily, as lawmakers remained divided over privacy concerns and over the qualifications of the acting intelligence chief. The lapse does not immediately halt all surveillance activity under the program, as previously collected data can still be used, but new collection under the authority is constrained. The situation developed alongside President Trump's announcement of plans to nominate Jay Clayton as director of national intelligence. Clayton is known primarily as a former chair of the Securities and Exchange Commission and has no publicly known intelligence background, a factor that has drawn scrutiny from some lawmakers. Section 702 has been reauthorized multiple times over the years, typically after contentious debate over how its use affects the privacy of American citizens whose communications are incidentally collected.

El Niño Officially Declared; Scientists Predict It Could Be Strongest of the Century

U.S. weather officials at NOAA have declared that El Niño has officially formed in the Pacific, with meteorologists predicting it could intensify to historic levels by fall. The UN secretary-general described the development as an urgent climate warning. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration has officially declared the onset of an El Niño climate pattern in the Pacific Ocean, and scientists are forecasting that it could become the strongest El Niño on record if it intensifies as projected through the fall. El Niño is a periodic warming of central and eastern Pacific surface temperatures that disrupts weather patterns worldwide, typically increasing the likelihood of drought in some regions, flooding in others, and driving global average temperatures higher. Meteorologists predict the current pattern could surpass previous benchmarks for intensity, which would amplify its effects on extreme weather globally. The United Nations secretary-general called the development an urgent climate warning. For the United States, a strong El Niño can affect precipitation patterns across the country, increase hurricane activity in the Pacific, and suppress Atlantic hurricane formation, while also influencing winter temperatures. Scientists note that the warming effect of El Niño is occurring on top of a baseline that is already elevated due to long-term climate change, which may amplify the overall impact.

Five Lupus Patients in England Achieve Remission Following Genetically Modified Cell Therapy Trial

Five patients with lupus in England are in remission after receiving CAR T-cell therapy in an NHS trial, in what doctors have described as a potential path toward a cure for the autoimmune disease. Five patients with lupus in England have entered remission following treatment with CAR T-cell therapy as part of a trial conducted through the National Health Service. CAR, or chimeric antigen receptor, T-cell therapy involves removing white blood cells from a patient, genetically modifying them to target specific disease-causing cells, and reinfusing them into the patient. The therapy has previously shown significant results in certain blood cancers, and the lupus trial represents an extension of the approach to autoimmune conditions. Doctors involved in the trial described the outcomes as a potential breakthrough, with the possibility of offering patients a long-term cure rather than ongoing disease management. Lupus is a chronic autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks healthy tissue, affecting multiple organ systems and disproportionately affecting women. The trial results are from a small patient group, and larger studies would be needed to confirm the therapy's safety and efficacy across a broader population.
