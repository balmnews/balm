Key US Surveillance Authority, FISA Section 702, Set to Expire as Congress Has Not Renewed It

FISA Section 702, a surveillance tool that officials say underpins more than 60% of the president's daily intelligence briefing, is set to lapse Friday as Congress has been unable to pass renewal legislation. A major US government surveillance authority known as FISA Section 702 is set to expire on Friday after Congress failed to pass legislation renewing it. The tool authorizes the collection of electronic communications from foreign targets overseas and is described by officials as a foundational component of American intelligence gathering. Government officials have stated that more than 60% of the content in the president's daily intelligence briefing draws on information collected under Section 702. The authority has existed in its current form since 2008 and has been renewed multiple times, but reauthorization efforts have been repeatedly complicated by disagreements between lawmakers over privacy protections and the scope of permissible searches of communications involving American citizens. The lapse does not immediately terminate all existing collection, but it would prevent the government from initiating new collection under the authority and could have cascading effects on intelligence operations over time. The expiration comes as the US is engaged in active military and diplomatic activity in the Middle East, increasing the operational stakes of a gap in surveillance capacity.

Five Lupus Patients in England Reach Remission Following Genetically Modified T-Cell Therapy Trial

Five lupus patients in England are in remission after receiving CAR T-cell therapy in an NHS trial, a development doctors describe as a potential route to a cure for the autoimmune disease. Five patients with lupus enrolled in an NHS clinical trial in England have entered remission after receiving CAR T-cell therapy, according to doctors involved in the trial. The treatment, known as chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy, involves removing a patient's own white blood cells, genetically modifying them to target specific disease-causing cells, and reinfusing them into the body. The approach has previously shown results in certain blood cancers, and this trial represents an application to autoimmune disease. Lupus is a chronic autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks healthy tissue, causing damage to organs including the kidneys, heart, and skin. There is currently no cure, and many patients require lifelong medication to manage symptoms. Doctors involved in the trial described the results as a potential medical breakthrough and said the therapy could eventually offer patients a cure rather than ongoing disease management. The trial remains at an early stage, and larger studies would be needed before the treatment could be considered for wider use.

Debate Grows Over Embryo Gene Editing as New Base-Editing Techniques Advance

Fertility specialists, biotech companies, and ethicists are divided over emerging base-editing techniques that can modify human embryo DNA, with supporters citing disease elimination and critics raising concerns about enhancement applications. Advances in a form of genetic modification called base editing are intensifying debate among fertility specialists, biotechnology companies, and ethicists about the future of editing human embryos. Unlike earlier gene-editing tools, base editing allows researchers to make precise changes to individual DNA letters without cutting the double helix, reducing the risk of unintended mutations. Proponents argue the technology could be used to eliminate serious inherited diseases before birth. Critics raise concerns that the same techniques could be applied not to prevent disease but to select or enhance non-medical traits, a prospect widely described as ethically fraught. The debate reflects longstanding tensions in the field that predate base editing, but the increased precision and accessibility of the newer techniques has given the discussion renewed urgency. Regulatory frameworks governing embryo editing vary significantly across countries, and no consensus exists on where the boundary between therapeutic and enhancement use should be drawn.

El Niño Officially Forms; Scientists Predict It Could Be the Strongest This Century

US weather officials at NOAA have confirmed El Niño has formed in the Pacific, with meteorologists predicting it could intensify to historic levels by autumn and drive extreme weather events globally. The climate pattern known as El Niño has officially formed in the Pacific Ocean, according to the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration. Meteorologists predict the pattern could intensify to become the strongest El Niño of the century, with potentially severe effects expected to emerge in the autumn months. El Niño typically causes sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific to rise above average, which can amplify weather extremes around the world, including more intense hurricanes in some regions, drought conditions in others, and above-average temperatures globally. The UN Secretary-General has described the development as an urgent climate warning. Scientists note that because El Niño would be layered on top of already elevated global temperatures driven by long-term climate change, its effects could be more pronounced than historical comparisons would suggest. The United States typically experiences altered precipitation patterns during strong El Niño years, including wetter conditions in the southern states and drought in parts of the Pacific Northwest.
