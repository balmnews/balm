Germany's AfD Wins Saxony-Anhalt State Election, Seeks to Form First Far-Right State Government Since World War II

Germany's Alternative for Germany party more than doubled its vote share in the Saxony-Anhalt state election, claiming a decisive victory. The result puts the party three seats short of a majority and positions it to seek a governing coalition, which would be the first far-right state government in Germany since 1945. The Alternative for Germany party, known as the AfD, claimed a decisive victory in Sunday's state election in Saxony-Anhalt, more than doubling its previous vote share in what multiple observers described as a historic result. The party fell three seats short of an outright majority in the state parliament and has called on other parties to work with it to form a government, arguing that democratic norms require it. No far-right party has led or formed a government in Germany since the end of World War II. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, whose center-right CDU party suffered a significant loss, said Monday he would remain in office despite the result. He appeared visibly shaken at a Berlin press conference. The AfD has been designated as an extremist organization by German domestic intelligence services, and Germany's major parties have long maintained a formal policy — sometimes called a firewall — against governing with the AfD. That consensus is now under pressure. The AfD's lead candidate in Saxony-Anhalt, Ulrich Siegmund, has built a large social media following, particularly among younger voters. AfD leaders thanked prominent international supporters following the result, and Russian officials praised the outcome. The vote is the first in a series of German regional elections this fall, and analysts say the result will intensify debate both within Germany and across Europe about how mainstream parties respond to far-right electoral gains. The result has no direct governing effect at the federal level, but it significantly increases pressure on the national parties ahead of those subsequent elections.
