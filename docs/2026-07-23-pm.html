The announcement comes at a moment of broader economic pressure, with oil prices surging past $100 a barrel due to the ongoing conflict with Iran. Economists and trading partners are assessing the combined impact of higher energy costs and new import duties on consumer prices and supply chains. No major trading partners had formally responded to the new tariffs at the time of publication.

Scientists within the Food and Drug Administration are recommending against broader availability of synthetic peptide injections, saying there is not enough evidence to support making them more widely accessible. The recommendation places agency career staff in tension with HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who has publicly supported allowing individuals greater freedom to use peptides and has described them favorably. Peptides are short chains of amino acids. The human body naturally produces peptides — including insulin and oxytocin — but the synthetic versions currently circulating in wellness and fitness communities are distinct from those naturally occurring compounds and are largely not approved by the FDA for clinical use. They have been promoted by influencers and some health practitioners for a range of purposes including muscle recovery, anti-aging, and performance enhancement. FDA career scientists argue that without adequate clinical trial data, the safety profile of many synthetic peptides is not established. The agency is currently reviewing the regulatory status of several compounds. The outcome of that review, and whether Kennedy's position within the administration influences the final regulatory approach, remains to be determined.
