British Prime Minister Keir Starmer announced his resignation on Monday, making him the sixth person to deliver a farewell speech outside 10 Downing Street in a decade. He will remain caretaker prime minister while the Labour Party selects his successor, a process expected to take several weeks. Financial markets reacted with relative calm to the announcement, though analysts noted continued uncertainty about the direction of economic policy under a new Labour government. US and Iran Agree to Roadmap for Nuclear Deal; Iran to Readmit Inspectors as Hormuz Reopens

Following talks in Switzerland, the US and Iran agreed to a framework that would see Iran allow UN nuclear inspectors to return and the Strait of Hormuz to reopen, in exchange for the lifting of US sanctions on Iranian oil exports. The two sides set a 60-day timeline to reach a final agreement. Following a round of negotiations in Switzerland, the United States and Iran agreed to a memorandum of understanding that includes Iran allowing UN nuclear inspectors back into the country, a partial lifting of US sanctions on Iranian oil exports, and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Vice President JD Vance, who led the US delegation, described the talks as laying a strong foundation for a lasting final deal, which the two sides set a 60-day target to complete. Secretary of State Marco Rubio was scheduled to begin a follow-on trip to the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, and Bahrain to discuss the framework with Gulf partners. The agreement draws broad comparisons to the 2015 nuclear accord that President Trump withdrew from in 2018. The framework is not without domestic opposition: former Vice President Mike Pence publicly described the deal as insufficient to address the Iranian threat, and several Republican foreign policy hawks in the Senate expressed concern that it concedes too much leverage. Analysts flagged a potential sticking point over the disposition of billions of dollars in previously frozen Iranian assets, with Iran having sent a delegation that included its central bank governor and oil ministry officials — a composition interpreted as signaling an immediate priority on financial relief. Energy market analysts noted that while the Hormuz reopening eases pressure on oil shipping, the 60-day negotiating window introduces ongoing uncertainty about whether a durable final agreement will be reached. For American consumers and businesses, the outcome of those negotiations will affect oil prices, global energy supply, and the broader diplomatic posture of the United States in the Middle East.

The Supreme Court issued a ruling in United States v. The decision favors the defendant in this case, but legal analysts note the ruling provides relatively little concrete guidance to lower courts on how to identify when the miscarriage-of-justice standard has been met. Courts will need to determine not just whether a party names the miscarriage-of-justice exception, but whether they can prove the factual basis for it. The ruling affects a common feature of the federal criminal justice system, where a substantial majority of convictions result from plea agreements that typically include such waivers. The precise scope of the decision and how broadly it will be applied across the federal circuits will likely take time to become clear through subsequent lower court interpretations.
