Measles Has Returned to Endemic Status in the United States

Federal health officials have confirmed that measles is officially endemic in the United States again, ending the elimination status the country held for decades. Regaining that status could take years and will require community-level vaccination strategies. The United States has lost its measles elimination status, with federal health authorities confirming that the disease has returned to sustained domestic transmission. Measles elimination — defined as the absence of continuous disease spread for 12 months or more — was first declared in the US in 2000, a landmark achievement of the country's childhood vaccination program. The return to endemic status reflects declining vaccination rates in some communities, driven by a combination of vaccine hesitancy, disruptions to routine childhood immunization during and after the pandemic, and policy changes affecting public health infrastructure. Officials have said regaining elimination status could take years, and that communities will need to adopt multiple strategies to protect vulnerable populations, including infants too young to be vaccinated and immunocompromised individuals. The development has significant implications for public health planning and for the US standing in global disease surveillance.

BepiColombo Spacecraft Arrives at Mercury After Eight-Year Journey

The joint European-Japanese BepiColombo mission has arrived at Mercury after traveling more than six billion miles over eight years. It will become only the second spacecraft in history to orbit the planet. The BepiColombo spacecraft, a joint mission of the European Space Agency and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency, has reached Mercury after a voyage of more than eight years and six billion miles. The spacecraft is preparing to enter orbit around Mercury, which would make it only the second mission in history to do so. The first was NASA's MESSENGER spacecraft, which orbited Mercury from 2011 to 2015. Mercury is the least explored of the terrestrial planets, in part because of the significant energy required to reach and orbit a body so close to the sun. BepiColombo carries two scientific orbiters that will study Mercury's surface, interior, magnetic field, and thin atmosphere in greater detail than any previous mission. The mission is expected to provide new data on the formation of the inner solar system and the history of rocky planets.

Neutral Atom Technology Accelerates Quantum Computing Race

A technology based on neutral atoms is rapidly advancing the competition to build a practical quantum computer, with many experts now believing a usable machine could arrive within this decade. A computing approach using what are called neutral atoms — individual atoms held in place by laser beams and used as quantum bits — has emerged as a leading contender in the race to build a practical quantum computer. Experts in the field increasingly believe that neutral atom systems could reach a level of utility within this decade, a faster timeline than many had previously anticipated. The approach competes with other quantum computing architectures, including superconducting qubits used by companies such as Google and IBM. Google researchers separately published a study this week demonstrating a phenomenon called quantum contextuality, in which a quantum machine's output can change based on context — an illustration of how quantum systems behave in ways that have no classical equivalent. The convergence of multiple technical advances is accelerating what has been a decades-long effort to build computers that can solve problems beyond the reach of conventional machines, with potential applications in drug discovery, materials science, cryptography, and optimization.
