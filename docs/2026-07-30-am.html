Research Finds Most 'Ovarian Cancer' Originates in Fallopian Tubes; Removal Can Cut Risk by 80%

The vast majority of cancers diagnosed as ovarian cancer actually originate in the fallopian tubes, and research indicates that removing the fallopian tubes can reduce the risk of developing the disease by nearly 80 percent. Medical research has established that most cancers classified as ovarian cancer begin in the fallopian tubes rather than the ovaries themselves. This distinction has significant implications for prevention: studies indicate that removing the fallopian tubes — a procedure called salpingectomy — can reduce a woman's risk of developing this cancer by close to 80 percent. The procedure is less invasive than removing the ovaries and does not trigger early menopause, making it a more practical preventive option for many patients. Medical guidelines in some countries have begun incorporating salpingectomy as a recommended option for women who have completed childbearing, particularly when undergoing other abdominal surgeries. Wider awareness of this research could meaningfully reduce rates of a disease that is often diagnosed late and carries a poor prognosis.
