US and Iran Exchange Strikes for Fifth Consecutive Day as Hormuz Tensions Continue

The US military launched additional strikes against Iranian military targets near the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, hours after Iran struck American military sites in Bahrain and Kuwait. Neither side has indicated willingness to de-escalate. The United States and Iran exchanged military strikes for a fifth consecutive day on Wednesday, deepening a conflict that has lasted more than four months. US Central Command described the American strikes as targeting Iranian military capabilities used to threaten vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian state media reported explosions in the port cities of Bandar Abbas and Chabahar. Earlier, Iran launched missiles at American military installations in Bahrain and Kuwait. Neither side has shown signs of backing down. The conflict began with an initial phase of strikes and was briefly paused by a memorandum of understanding signed on June 17, but both sides have since moved away from that agreement. President Trump, when asked whether he would escalate further, said he had not yet decided whether to, in his words, finish off Iran, and separately warned that Iran had better behave. Trump also withdrew a proposal to impose transit tolls on vessels passing through the Strait of Hormuz, a reversal that the international shipping industry described as adding to uncertainty for operators. Israel, which views the outcome of the US-Iran conflict as directly affecting its own security, has remained on the sidelines, with Israeli officials reportedly preferring continued hostilities over any agreement that does not address Iranian threats to Israel. The Strait of Hormuz handles a significant portion of the world's oil shipments, and prolonged military activity in the region has implications for global energy prices and supply chains affecting American consumers.
