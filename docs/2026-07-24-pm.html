Study Links Early Exposure to PFAS Chemicals to Gut Inflammation in Children

New research found that prenatal and early childhood exposure to mixtures of PFAS chemicals is likely associated with higher levels of intestinal inflammation in children, which can contribute to conditions including Crohn's disease and colorectal cancer. A new study — described as the first of its kind — found that prenatal and early life exposure to mixtures of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, commonly known as PFAS or forever chemicals, is likely associated with elevated levels of intestinal inflammation in children. Intestinal inflammation is a known contributor to conditions including ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, and colorectal cancer. PFAS are a broad class of synthetic chemicals used in a wide range of consumer and industrial products, from nonstick cookware to food packaging. They are called forever chemicals because they break down extremely slowly and accumulate in the environment and human tissue. Previous research had established links between PFAS exposure and a range of health outcomes in adults; this study specifically examines the pediatric gut as a pathway of concern. The findings add to a growing body of evidence supporting tighter regulation of PFAS in consumer products and water supplies.

The European Commission fined Google €890 million for breaching EU competition rules by giving preference to its own services — including shopping and hotel comparisons — over rivals in search results and its app store. The European Commission fined Google a total of €890 million, or approximately £760 million, for violations of the Digital Markets Act. The ruling follows a pattern of EU enforcement actions against major US technology companies.
