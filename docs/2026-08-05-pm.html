Most Detailed Images of the Sun's Surface Reveal Previously Unseen Features

Astronomers using the Inouye solar telescope in Hawaii have captured the most detailed images ever taken of the Sun's surface, revealing new structures including swirling vortices and golden bands smaller than 20 kilometers across. Researchers using the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope in Hawaii, the world's most powerful solar telescope, have captured unprecedented images of the Sun's surface showing features that have never been observed before. The images reveal swirling vortices and golden banded structures at scales smaller than 20 kilometers across, offering a level of detail not previously achievable. Scientists say these new observations may help explain one of solar physics' most persistent mysteries: why the Sun's outer atmosphere, the corona, is dramatically hotter than its visible surface, a phenomenon that has puzzled researchers for decades. The Inouye telescope, which began science operations in recent years, is designed to observe the Sun in finer detail than any previous instrument. The findings are expected to inform models of solar weather and space weather forecasting, which has practical implications for satellite operations, power grids, and other infrastructure on Earth.
