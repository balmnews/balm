NOAA Budget Cuts Reduce U.S. Capacity to Forecast and Respond to El Niño

Former government officials and scientists say cuts to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration have reduced the United States' ability to forecast and respond to extreme weather as a potentially significant El Niño develops. Significant budget and staffing reductions at the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration — the federal agency responsible for weather forecasting, climate research, and disaster response — have materially reduced the country's capacity to prepare for and respond to extreme weather events, according to former officials and scientists. The concern is heightened by the development of what researchers describe as a potentially significant El Niño cycle, a periodic ocean-warming pattern associated with flooding, drought, and severe storms across different regions of the United States. NOAA manages the satellite systems, ocean buoys, and research programs that feed data into weather models used by emergency managers and the public. Scientists say the reductions have stalled ongoing research and degraded forecast quality at a time when accurate prediction is particularly consequential for communities in flood- and drought-prone areas. The administration has not publicly addressed the specific capability concerns raised.

Student-Led Movement Ends 14-Year Rule of Serbia's Dominant Party in Election

A student-led political movement won a decisive election victory in Serbia, ending 14 years of rule by Aleksandar Vucic's party. Vucic conceded defeat. A political movement rooted in Serbia's student protest movement won a landslide victory in Serbian elections, defeating the ruling party of Aleksandar Vucic after 14 years in power. Vucic conceded the result. The outcome is a significant political realignment in a country of strategic importance to the European Union and to broader regional stability in the western Balkans. The newly victorious movement, known as the Student List, emerged from months of large-scale street demonstrations that had focused on issues including government accountability and infrastructure safety. Observers note that the movement has not articulated a detailed governing agenda, leaving questions about the direction of Serbian foreign policy — including its posture toward NATO, the EU, and Russia — unresolved. Serbia's relationship with the West and with Russia has been a persistent geopolitical concern for the United States and European partners.

Federal Reserve Holds Interest Rates Steady; Plans to Reduce Number of Annual Rate Meetings

The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate unchanged at its October meeting, the last before midterm elections. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh also announced the central bank will reduce the number of rate-setting meetings per year beginning in 2027. The Federal Reserve's Open Market Committee voted to hold interest rates steady at its October meeting, leaving rates at one of the highest pre-election levels in more than two decades. Two of the twelve board members dissented, preferring an immediate quarter-point increase. Officials acknowledged that inflation remains elevated. Markets widely expect the Fed to raise rates at its next meeting in December. Separately, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh announced a structural change: beginning in 2027, the Fed will hold seven rate-setting meetings per year, down from the traditional eight, and six in 2028. The rate hold comes at a politically sensitive moment, one week before midterm elections, though the Fed is legally independent of the political calendar. For American consumers and borrowers, the decision means no immediate change to mortgage rates, auto loan rates, or credit card rates tied to the federal funds rate — though the December meeting remains a live event for a potential increase.

Record High Tides Flood Coastal Communities from San Diego to San Francisco Bay Area

Record-breaking tidal surges inundated coastal communities along the entire California coast this week, flooding streets, homes, and businesses from San Diego to the San Francisco Bay Area. Historic high tides struck California's coastline this week, producing record water levels at multiple points from San Diego in the south to the San Francisco Bay Area in the north. Seawater surged into low-lying neighborhoods, submerging streets and flooding homes and businesses. Some residents in heavily affected areas improvised with kayaks and other watercraft to navigate inundated roads. Authorities in multiple communities issued warnings and some evacuation guidance for the most vulnerable low-lying areas. The event is consistent with projections that king tides — the highest tides of the year — will become more extreme over time due to rising baseline sea levels. For California, which has hundreds of miles of low-elevation coastal development, the flooding illustrates the near-term practical risks of sea level rise affecting infrastructure, property, and emergency response systems.

AI Tools Uncovering Large Numbers of Software Security Flaws, Creating Pressure to Patch

Artificial intelligence systems are identifying software vulnerabilities at a scale that is outpacing engineers' ability to address them, raising concerns that unpatched flaws could be exploited before fixes are applied. Artificial intelligence tools are uncovering software security flaws at a volume and speed that is straining engineering teams across Silicon Valley and the broader technology industry. The volume of newly identified vulnerabilities is large enough that companies are racing to prioritize and fix them before malicious actors can exploit them. Security researchers note that the same AI capabilities that identify flaws can also be used by hackers to find and take advantage of them, making the speed of the patching process particularly important. The development marks a significant shift in the security landscape: while software vulnerability scanning has existed for decades, AI has dramatically accelerated the rate at which flaws are discovered, changing the economics and timelines of security response for technology companies and the organizations that rely on their products.

HHS Overrides FDA to Allow Compounding Pharmacies to Produce Six Unapproved Peptide Injections

The Department of Health and Human Services announced it will allow compounding pharmacies to produce six peptide injections that have not been approved by the FDA, overriding the agency's advice against the move. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. announced that small compounding pharmacies will be permitted to produce and dispense six peptide injections that have not received Food and Drug Administration approval. The FDA had advised against the move. The peptides in question have been widely promoted by social media influencers and adopted within wellness and fitness communities, though they have not undergone the standard FDA clinical trial and safety review process. Compounding pharmacies are specialized facilities that mix or alter medications, typically for patients with specific needs, and are subject to different regulatory requirements than manufacturers of FDA-approved drugs. The announcement represents a unilateral policy declaration by the HHS secretary, and it is unclear what regulatory mechanism will govern production standards, dosing, or safety monitoring for these products. Health professionals have previously raised concerns about the lack of clinical evidence for many peptide therapies.
