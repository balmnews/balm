United States Loses Measles Elimination Status After More Than 5,400 Cases and 10 Deaths

International health officials have determined the United States has lost its measles elimination status, held for 26 years, after reporting more than 5,400 cases and 10 measles-related deaths — the highest death toll since the early 1990s. The United States has officially lost its measles elimination status, a designation it had maintained for 26 years, following a record number of cases and fatalities. States have reported more than 5,400 measles cases, and 10 measles-related deaths have been recorded — the most since the early 1990s. A country is considered to have eliminated measles when it has interrupted continuous transmission of the virus for at least 12 months; the sustained spread of cases over the past year has ended that status for the US. Regaining elimination status typically takes years and requires documented interruption of transmission. Measles poses risks beyond the immediate infection: the virus can damage the immune system's memory, reducing a person's ability to fight off other diseases for months or years after recovery. This effect, sometimes called immune amnesia, means measles outbreaks can have health consequences extending well beyond the cases counted. Public health officials are urging vaccination, as the MMR vaccine is highly effective at preventing infection. Health authorities and researchers are examining what combination of vaccination rate declines, geographic clustering, and other factors drove this year's resurgence. Communities with lower vaccination rates face elevated ongoing risk.
