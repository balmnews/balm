The COP17 biodiversity summit in Armenia concluded with countries renewing commitments to protect nature but without agreement on how to fund those commitments. Critics say the summit format is failing to produce results. The COP17 UN biodiversity summit, held in Yerevan, Armenia, concluded with governments renewing pledges to protect wildlife and restore ecosystems but without resolving persistent disagreements over financing. The question of how developing nations will be compensated for conservation efforts — and who will pay — remained unanswered at the end of two weeks of negotiations. Critics and conservation groups said the outcome continues a decades-long pattern in which targets are set but not met, noting that in the 34-year history of the Convention on Biological Diversity, no major target has been fully achieved. Multiple sources highlighted calls for structural reform of the summit process, with some observers arguing the current consensus-based rules allow a small number of holdout countries to stall progress. Countries had previously committed under the Kunming-Montreal framework to protect 30 percent of the planet's land and oceans by 2030, but implementation and funding remain in dispute.
