US Government Auctions 31.5 Million Acres of Pacific Seabed for Deep-Sea Mining Exploration

The federal government conducted its first-ever deep-sea mining lease auction, selling rights to 31.5 million acres of Pacific Ocean seabed to Wetstone US Ventures for $6 million. The sale proceeded despite a federal judge's objections and covers mapping and preparation work, not active mining. The United States government completed its first-ever auction of deep-sea mining rights, selling a lease covering 31.5 million acres of Pacific Ocean seabed to a company called Wetstone US Ventures for $6 million. The sale took place over the objections of a federal judge. The lease covers activities including seabed mapping and site preparation, and does not authorize actual mineral extraction at this stage. Deep-sea mining involves extracting mineral nodules from the ocean floor, which contain metals including cobalt, nickel, and manganese used in batteries and electronics. The practice remains scientifically controversial, with marine biologists and environmental groups raising concerns about damage to deep-sea ecosystems that are poorly understood. The administration's decision to proceed despite judicial objections and to conduct the first such sale in US history marks a significant policy shift in federal ocean resource management.

A new report prepared for the US Congress warns that supplying nuclear-powered submarines to Australia under the AUKUS agreement would diminish the US Navy's own fleet for an extended period, and raises questions about whether Australian-flagged submarines could be compelled into service in a conflict with China. A report prepared for the US Congress has raised significant concerns about the AUKUS submarine agreement, under which the United States agreed to provide nuclear-powered submarines to Australia. The report warns that transferring submarines to Australia would reduce the operational capacity of the US Navy's own fleet for what it describes as "many years." It also questions a central strategic rationale of the arrangement: whether Australian-flagged vessels could legally or practically be required to participate in a conflict with China. The US Navy's latest shipbuilding plan was cited as context for the fleet capacity concerns. The AUKUS partnership, involving Australia, the United Kingdom, and the United States, was announced in 2021 and has been a cornerstone of the Biden and Trump administrations' Indo-Pacific strategy. The report does not recommend canceling the agreement but introduces new legislative scrutiny at a moment when the deal's implementation timeline and costs are already subjects of debate in all three countries.

A clinical trial of a drug called daraxonrasib, taken as a daily pill, has shown the treatment can shrink tumors and in some patients appeared to eliminate them entirely in cases of pancreatic cancer. Pancreatic cancer has one of the lowest survival rates of any cancer type, in part because it is typically detected at an advanced stage and has few effective treatment options. The trial results were described as significant by researchers, though the drug has not yet received regulatory approval and larger confirmatory trials would be needed before it could become a standard treatment. The findings represent a potential advance in a disease area that has seen limited progress over decades.
