The United States has officially lost its measles elimination status, a designation it held since 2000, as federal officials work to formulate a plan to regain it. Measles elimination status requires that the virus not circulate continuously for more than twelve months; sustained outbreaks in recent years have undermined that threshold. Public health experts describe regaining the status as a significant challenge, requiring sustained vaccination campaigns and improved outbreak response. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. had, in earlier stages of the current outbreaks, publicly downplayed the severity of measles and questioned aspects of the standard public health response. He now faces the responsibility of leading the federal effort to reverse the disease's spread. Measles is preventable through the MMR vaccine, which has a well-established safety record. Health officials continue to recommend vaccination as the primary tool for controlling transmission. The loss of elimination status does not mean measles is endemic in the US, but it marks a public health regression that experts say will require a coordinated national response to address.

Levent Alpöge, a Turkish-American mathematician employed at Anthropic, posted a claimed proof of the Riemann Hypothesis on Friday, stating that the company's AI system Claude played a meaningful role in the work. Two independent review groups have since examined the proof and said the claim appears to be valid, lending it more credibility than is typical at such an early stage. The Riemann Hypothesis, first posed in 1859, concerns the distribution of prime numbers and is considered one of the most important unsolved problems in mathematics. It is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems identified by the Clay Mathematics Institute, each carrying a one-million-dollar prize. A verified proof would have significant implications for number theory and potentially for fields such as cryptography that depend on properties of prime numbers. The claim has not yet completed full peer review, and mathematical proofs of this complexity typically require extended scrutiny before being accepted by the broader community. Still, the early independent verification has drawn substantial attention in both the mathematics and AI research communities, as it would represent a landmark use of AI as a tool in advanced mathematical discovery.
