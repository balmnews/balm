Trump Administration Cancels $810 Million in Congressional Spending Using Rare Rescission Authority

The Trump administration used a rarely invoked budget mechanism to cancel approximately $810 million in spending previously approved by Congress, with most cuts targeting refugee programs and services for unaccompanied minors. The Trump administration has revoked nearly $810 million in congressionally approved spending by invoking a little-used budget authority known as a pocket rescission. The funds targeted include programs administered by the Department of Health and Human Services that serve refugees, unaccompanied minors, diversity initiatives, and migrant education. Under the rescission process, the administration can cancel spending that Congress has approved if it acts within a specific window before funds are formally obligated. Congressional Democrats have raised objections, arguing the maneuver circumvents legislative authority over appropriations. The administration has argued the cuts align with its broader priorities on immigration and government spending. The use of this mechanism is notable because it bypasses the standard process by which the executive branch requests that Congress rescind funds, instead unilaterally canceling them. Legal challenges to the action are considered possible, given ongoing disputes over the administration's authority to impound or redirect congressionally approved funds.
