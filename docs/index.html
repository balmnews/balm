Report: Deportees Are Being Transferred to Third Countries Despite U.S. Court Orders Blocking Direct Removal

A pattern has emerged in which immigrants who hold U.S. court orders protecting them from deportation to specific countries are nonetheless ending up in those countries through an indirect route: the Trump administration deports them to a third country, which then transfers them to the nation the U.S. is prohibited from directly sending them to. This practice, known in international law as refoulement when it results in return to a place of potential persecution or harm, has drawn legal and human rights scrutiny. The reporting indicates that foreign partner governments are cooperating with these arrangements. U.S. courts have issued orders in individual cases prohibiting direct removal to certain countries, typically because of documented risks of persecution, torture, or other serious harm. The indirect transfer method effectively circumvents those orders without formally violating the letter of domestic court rulings. Immigration lawyers and advocates have raised constitutional and international law objections to the practice. The administration has not publicly addressed the specific mechanism described in the reporting.
