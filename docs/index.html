Two studies challenge conventional dietary guidance on dairy. Two recent studies raise questions about longstanding dietary recommendations regarding dairy products. The first study found that consuming three daily servings of full-fat dairy over a 12-week period did not negatively affect participants' weight, cholesterol levels, or insulin resistance. Researchers also observed improvements in blood pressure and nutrient intake among participants, results the study authors say warrant a reassessment of guidance that has discouraged full-fat dairy consumption for decades. A separate study examined the nutritional consequences of replacing conventional dairy products with plant-based alternatives such as oat milk, vegan cheese, and dairy-free yogurt. That research found that even a partial shift toward plant-based dairy substitutes could lead to meaningful reductions in iodine, calcium, riboflavin, and vitamin B12 — nutrients for which dairy is a primary dietary source in many Western diets. The researchers noted that young people and women may be particularly at risk of deficiencies from such substitutions. Both studies add to an ongoing scientific discussion about how dietary guidelines should handle dairy, and neither is likely to be the final word on the subject.
