Supreme Court Allows Alabama to Use Congressional Map That Removes Majority-Black District

The Supreme Court ruled 6-3 to allow Alabama to use a redrawn congressional map that eliminates one of the state's two majority-Black districts for the 2026 midterm elections, overriding a lower court finding that the map was racially discriminatory. The Supreme Court issued an emergency ruling allowing Alabama to proceed with a congressional map that eliminates one of its two majority-Black voting districts for the 2026 midterm elections. The decision was 6-3 along ideological lines. A lower federal court had previously struck down the map, finding it racially discriminatory under the Voting Rights Act. The Supreme Court's emergency order sets aside that lower court ruling while the legal challenge proceeds. Under the approved map, Alabama will have six congressional districts that lean Republican and one that leans Democratic. The ruling is the latest in a long-running legal dispute over Alabama's congressional boundaries that has reached the Supreme Court multiple times in recent years. Civil rights advocates argued the map dilutes Black voting power in violation of federal law. The decision has implications for the balance of power in Congress, as the outcome of competitive House races in November may affect which party controls the chamber. It also continues a pattern of the current court limiting the scope of the Voting Rights Act in redistricting cases.
