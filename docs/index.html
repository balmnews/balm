Trump Signs New Executive Orders Targeting Birthright Citizenship and Birth Tourism

President Trump signed two executive orders seeking to further restrict birthright citizenship and deny visas to foreign nationals entering the US to give birth, weeks after the Supreme Court rejected a broader earlier attempt. President Trump signed two new executive orders aimed at limiting birthright citizenship and curbing what the administration calls 'birth tourism.' The orders deny visas to foreign nationals believed to be entering the country primarily to give birth, and expand the existing definition of people whose US-born children would not be eligible for automatic citizenship. The actions come roughly five weeks after the Supreme Court rejected a broader earlier executive order that would have denied automatic citizenship to children born to undocumented immigrants and temporary foreign residents. That prior order was found to conflict with the Fourteenth Amendment's birthright citizenship guarantee. The new orders take a narrower approach. The ACLU and other legal advocates said the new orders remain constitutionally vulnerable and are likely to face court challenges. Trump said he believed the new actions would withstand legal scrutiny. The administration did not provide detailed legal analysis of how the orders differ from the one the Court rejected. Birthright citizenship — the constitutional guarantee that anyone born on US soil is automatically a citizen — has been a persistent focus of Trump's immigration agenda across both his terms in office.

The Federal Communications Commission has eliminated a rule that prevented any single owner from controlling broadcast television stations that collectively reach more than 39 percent of US households. The rule, in place for decades, was designed to limit concentration of ownership in the broadcast television industry and preserve local media diversity. Its removal clears a regulatory path for significant consolidation among television station groups. Critics of the move argue it could reduce the number of independent local news operations and concentrate media ownership in fewer hands. Supporters contend the rule was outdated given the shift in how Americans consume news and entertainment across cable, streaming, and digital platforms. The change takes effect under the current FCC, which has moved to roll back several media ownership restrictions.

FDA Approves Moderna's mRNA-Based Flu Vaccine for Older Adults

The FDA has approved Moderna's mRNA-based influenza vaccine, the first of its kind, targeted at older adults and expected to be available for the upcoming flu season. The Food and Drug Administration has approved Moderna's mRNA-based influenza vaccine, marking the first time the technology used in COVID-19 vaccines has been authorized for use against the flu. The vaccine is specifically targeted at older adults, a population that tends to experience more severe influenza outcomes. Moderna says the vaccine will be available in time for the upcoming flu season. The FDA had initially declined to review the application but reversed course following public pressure. The approval represents a significant expansion of mRNA vaccine technology beyond COVID-19. mRNA vaccines work by instructing cells to produce a protein that triggers an immune response, without using a live or inactivated virus. Supporters of the technology say it can be updated more quickly than traditional flu vaccines as circulating strains change each year.

Discarded SpaceX Rocket Debris Crashes into the Moon, Creating New Crater

A four-tonne piece of a discarded SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket traveling at approximately 8,690 kilometers per hour struck the moon, creating a new crater. A telescope in Chile and South Korea's lunar orbiter both captured evidence of the impact. A four-tonne discarded piece of a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket unintentionally crashed into the moon after drifting in space since last year. The object, roughly the height of a five-story building, struck the lunar surface at approximately 8,690 kilometers per hour, or about 5,400 miles per hour. The impact posed no danger to Earth. The European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope in Chile captured spectral evidence of the collision, detecting sodium and lithium gas released from the impact — described as 'spectral lines' — along with a large plume of debris. South Korea's lunar orbiter captured before-and-after images of the moon's surface showing the newly formed crater. Uncontrolled rocket bodies occasionally end up on impact trajectories with the moon or other bodies after completing their missions, and this event has renewed discussion about guidelines for disposing of spent space hardware.

Scientists Use Artificial Intelligence to Design and Build Novel Viruses

Scientists have for the first time created functional viruses whose genetic sequences were designed entirely by artificial intelligence, raising both medical hopes and biosecurity concerns. Scientists have successfully created functional viruses with genetic sequences designed from scratch by artificial intelligence, marking what researchers describe as a significant milestone in both medicine and biosecurity. The 16 viruses created are bacteriophages — viruses that infect bacteria rather than humans or animals — and are already used in medicine and food safety applications around the world. Researchers say the ability to design novel bacteriophages using AI could accelerate the development of treatments for antibiotic-resistant bacterial infections, an urgent global health challenge. However, the achievement also raises concerns about the potential for the same technology to be misused to engineer harmful pathogens. Scientists and biosecurity experts are calling for careful governance frameworks to ensure the technology is developed safely. This is considered the first confirmed instance of AI-designed viruses being synthesized and shown to function.
