Study Finds Switching Fully or Partly to Plant-Based Dairy Alternatives May Reduce Key Micronutrients

A new study found that replacing dairy products with plant-based alternatives — even partially — can reduce intake of iodine, calcium, riboflavin, and vitamin B12, with young people and women at greatest risk of deficiency. Researchers found that substituting dairy milk, cheese, and yogurt with plant-based alternatives is associated with reduced intake of several micronutrients important to human health, including iodine, calcium, riboflavin, and vitamin B12. The study found the effect was present even among people who made only partial switches to vegan alternatives, not just those who eliminated dairy entirely. The researchers identified young people and women as the groups most likely to experience deficiency as a result of the dietary shift. Iodine in particular is important for thyroid function and fetal brain development during pregnancy, and dairy is one of the primary sources of iodine in many Western diets. Many plant-based alternatives are not fortified to the same degree as dairy products. The study's authors recommended that people who reduce dairy consumption pay attention to alternative sources of these nutrients, or consider supplementation.
