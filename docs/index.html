Regaining that status is expected to take years and will require coordinated vaccination and public health efforts across communities. The United States has officially lost its measles elimination status, a designation that had been held since 2000 and required no sustained transmission of the disease for twelve consecutive months. The loss of that status reflects a sustained increase in measles cases driven primarily by declining vaccination rates in certain communities. Measles elimination does not mean the disease is eradicated globally — cases continue in many countries — but it means the virus is no longer being continuously transmitted domestically without importation. Regaining elimination status typically takes years of sustained public health effort, including increasing vaccination coverage, rapid identification and containment of outbreaks, and ongoing community-level interventions. The MMR vaccine is highly effective and widely available. Public health officials are calling for communities with lower vaccination rates to increase coverage as the primary path back to elimination status.
