A new drug called bepirovirsen showed the ability to achieve what researchers described as a functional cure in approximately one in five patients with chronic hepatitis B in clinical trial results. Chronic hepatitis B affects hundreds of millions of people globally and carries serious long-term health risks including liver cancer. Scientists have reported that an experimental drug, bepirovirsen, produced outcomes in a clinical trial that some researchers described as potentially curative for a portion of patients with chronic hepatitis B — a long-term viral infection that most people recover from, but which poses significant risks including cirrhosis and liver cancer for those who do not. The trial found that roughly one in five patients achieved a functional cure, meaning the virus was suppressed to undetectable levels in a durable way. Chronic hepatitis B affects an estimated 300 million people worldwide, and current treatments manage but do not eliminate the infection, making a potential cure medically significant.
