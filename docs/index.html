52 US Maternity Wards Have Closed Since Federal Medicaid Cuts Were Announced

Experts attribute the trend directly to reduced federal funding. Analysis by the nonprofit organization Protect Our Care found that 52 maternity wards across the United States have closed since the HR1 bill, which included significant Medicaid funding reductions, was announced in July 2025. A further eight closures are reported to be pending. Healthcare experts and hospital administrators have attributed the wave of closures to the financial pressure created by reduced Medicaid reimbursements, which fund a substantial share of maternity care in the US, particularly in rural and lower-income communities. Maternity ward closures are associated with increased distances patients must travel for delivery care, which research has linked to worse outcomes for mothers and newborns. The trend has been especially pronounced in rural areas where hospitals operate on thin margins and rely heavily on Medicaid. The closures represent a measurable policy consequence of the funding changes that lawmakers and health advocates are now pointing to as midterm elections approach.
