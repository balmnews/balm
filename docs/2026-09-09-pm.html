PFAS are a broad class of synthetic chemicals used for decades in firefighting foam at military bases, as well as in various industrial and consumer products. They are called "forever chemicals" because they do not break down easily in the environment or in the human body. Studies have linked PFAS exposure to a range of health conditions, including certain cancers, immune system effects, and developmental problems in children. Communities near military bases where PFAS have contaminated groundwater or drinking water sources face continued exposure during the delay period. The Pentagon has cited budget constraints and logistical complexity as factors in the postponements. Congressional members from both parties have pressed the Defense Department for faster action, reflecting constituent concern about health risks in communities adjacent to affected installations.
