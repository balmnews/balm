The CDC has confirmed more than 4,000 measles cases in the United States this year, the largest outbreak in 35 years. Nearly one in five cases involves children under five, and 95 percent of cases are among unvaccinated individuals. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has confirmed over 4,000 measles cases across the United States in 2026, marking the country's largest outbreak of the highly contagious virus in 35 years. Physicians in affected areas are urging vaccination as the primary tool to stop transmission. Nearly 20 percent of confirmed cases involve children under the age of five, a population at elevated risk for severe complications including pneumonia and encephalitis. Notably, 95 percent of all cases are occurring among people who are unvaccinated, reinforcing public health guidance that the MMR vaccine provides strong protection. Measles was declared eliminated in the United States in 2000, but declining vaccination rates in some communities have created pockets of susceptibility that allow the virus to spread. Parents and caregivers who are unsure of their children's vaccination status are advised to contact their healthcare provider.

New Strain of Candida auris Resistant to All Major Antifungal Drugs Found in U.K. Patient

A U.K. hospital patient developed an infection caused by a strain of Candida auris resistant to all four major classes of antifungal drugs, only the second known case of total drug resistance in this fungal pathogen. Researchers have identified a strain of Candida auris in a hospitalized patient in the United Kingdom that is resistant to all four major classes of antifungal drugs, a condition known as pan-resistance. This is only the second documented case of pan-resistant C. auris anywhere in the world. Candida auris is a fungal pathogen of significant public health concern because it spreads easily in healthcare settings, is difficult to identify with standard laboratory tests, and has shown a consistent ability to develop resistance to the drugs used to treat it. The patient, a 28-year-old woman, acquired the infection while hospitalized. The case highlights the capacity of C. auris to evolve resistance rapidly, and researchers say it underscores the urgency of developing new antifungal treatments. The finding is relevant to American healthcare settings, where C. auris infections have been reported in hospitals across multiple states.

Researchers Say Online Ads Used to Deploy 'Zero-Click' Spyware on Target Devices

Researchers at the University of Toronto's Citizen Lab say an Israeli advertising company has been serving online ads that enable spyware installation on targeted devices without any action required from the user. Citizen Lab, a digital investigations unit at the University of Toronto, has published findings alleging that an Israeli advertising agency has been distributing online advertisements that function as delivery mechanisms for so-called zero-click spyware. Unlike conventional spyware that requires a target to click a malicious link, zero-click spyware can be installed simply by an advertisement loading on a device, requiring no interaction from the user. Researchers say they believe dozens of devices were compromised through this method, with targets identified among the ad network's reach of millions of users. The findings raise significant concerns for everyday internet users, as the attack vector — standard online advertising — is one of the most common elements of web browsing. Citizen Lab did not disclose the identities of specific targets. The Israeli firm named in the report had not publicly responded to the allegations at the time of publication.
