Trump Administration Seeks to Remove Race and Ethnicity Questions From US Census

The Trump administration is pursuing the removal of race and ethnicity questions from the next US census, a change that would affect how federal data is collected and used for policy, redistricting, and civil rights enforcement. The Trump administration has proposed eliminating questions about race and ethnicity from the next United States census, which is scheduled for 2030. Race and ethnicity data collected by the census has been used for a wide range of purposes, including the enforcement of civil rights laws, the allocation of federal funding, and the drawing of legislative district boundaries. The census has included some form of race-related question since 1790, though the framing and categories have changed substantially over time — initially to exclude people of color from full civic participation, and more recently to support inclusion and anti-discrimination efforts. The proposal would represent a significant departure from decades of census practice. Civil rights advocates have raised concerns that removing the questions would make it harder to document and address racial disparities in areas such as housing, education, and healthcare. The administration has not provided a final timeline for implementing the change.
