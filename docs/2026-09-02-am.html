UN Report Says Carbon Removal Must Now Accompany Emissions Cuts to Avert Worst Climate Outcomes

A new UN report concludes that global climate targets have been missed and that carbon removal at scale is now required alongside emissions reductions to limit the most severe warming outcomes. A new report from the United Nations concludes that previously established climate goals have not been met, and that the world must now pursue active removal of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere in addition to cutting ongoing emissions. The report describes carbon removal combined with emissions reduction as the only remaining viable pathway to prevent warming at levels the scientific community considers catastrophic. Carbon removal can refer to a range of approaches, from tree planting and soil carbon storage to direct air capture technologies that chemically extract CO2 from the atmosphere. None of these methods currently operate anywhere near the scale the report says is needed. The findings reflect a broader shift in scientific consensus: early climate strategies assumed emissions reductions alone would be sufficient if implemented quickly enough, but the pace of reduction has not matched projections, leaving a gap that the report argues can only be closed through removal. The report adds urgency to ongoing international debates about who bears the cost of carbon removal and how it should be governed.
