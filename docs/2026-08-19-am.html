Trump Administration Plans to Repeal 25-Year Roadless Rule, Opening 45 Million Forest Acres

The Trump administration announced plans to rescind the "roadless rule," a 25-year-old federal regulation that has protected nearly 45 million acres of national forest from road construction, logging, and drilling. The Trump administration announced it intends to repeal the "roadless rule," a federal regulation in place for 25 years that has restricted road construction, logging, and resource extraction in approximately 45 million acres of national forest land. The rule was established to protect old-growth and roadless backcountry areas within the national forest system. Its repeal would open these lands to commercial timber operations, energy development, and road building. The announcement applies broadly across national forests in multiple states and represents one of the most significant changes to federal public lands management in a generation. Environmental groups are expected to mount legal challenges, while the timber and energy industries have long sought relief from the rule's restrictions. The administration framed the move as part of a broader effort to expand domestic resource production. The rule has survived multiple previous attempts at revision or repeal, having withstood legal and administrative challenges over the past two and a half decades.
