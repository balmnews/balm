Andy Burnham Becomes UK Prime Minister, Promising Economic Reform and Public Ownership

He has pledged public ownership of utilities, expanded North Sea fossil fuel drilling, and measures to reduce living costs. Andy Burnham was formally appointed as the United Kingdom's prime minister on Monday, succeeding Keir Starmer and becoming the seventh person to hold the office in a decade. Burnham, the leader of the Labour Party, built his political profile as the long-serving mayor of Greater Manchester. In his first speech as prime minister, he said Britain must be honest about the challenges it faces, while pledging to deliver what he called good growth in every part of the country. His stated agenda leans notably leftward compared to recent Labour leadership: he has pledged to bring utilities into public ownership, allow expanded North Sea fossil fuel drilling, and take measures to ease the cost of living. Burnham's appointment comes as Britain contends with persistent weak economic growth, high living costs, and significant regional inequality. He is the first prime minister in the current era with deep roots in England's north, a fact his supporters say will shape his governing priorities.

2.6 Million Americans Lost Health Insurance After ACA Subsidy Expiration

An estimated 2.6 million Americans lost health insurance coverage after enhanced Affordable Care Act subsidies expired. The coverage losses are expected to result in delayed medical care, increased emergency room use, and higher out-of-pocket costs for affected families. The expiration of enhanced premium subsidies under the Affordable Care Act has left an estimated 2.6 million Americans without health insurance, according to new analysis. The enhanced subsidies, which had expanded coverage access significantly, were not renewed, and the resulting coverage losses are concentrated among lower- and middle-income households. Health policy analysts note that the consequences of losing insurance coverage tend to materialize gradually — people defer routine care, avoid specialist visits, and delay treatment until conditions worsen. This often leads to higher-cost emergency care and worse health outcomes over time, and places additional strain on household budgets when medical bills arrive. The lapse represents one of the largest single coverage reductions since the ACA's passage, and comes as Congress continues to debate the future of health care subsidy levels.

Legionnaires' Disease Outbreak on NYC's Upper East Side Kills Second Person, Infects 72

A Legionnaires' disease outbreak centered on Manhattan's Upper East Side has killed two people and infected 72, according to New York City health officials. New York City health officials have reported a second death linked to a Legionnaires' disease outbreak on Manhattan's Upper East Side. A total of 72 people have been confirmed infected in the outbreak. Legionnaires' disease is a severe form of pneumonia caused by the Legionella bacterium, which typically spreads through contaminated water systems such as cooling towers, hot tubs, and large plumbing systems. It does not spread person-to-person. Older adults, people with weakened immune systems, and smokers face the highest risk of serious illness. City health officials are investigating the source of the contamination. Symptoms include fever, cough, shortness of breath, and muscle aches, and the illness can be treated with antibiotics if caught early.
