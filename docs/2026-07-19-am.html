US Eliminates Automatic Protections for Newly Listed Threatened Species

The US government has ended the policy of automatically extending protections to animals newly listed as threatened under the Endangered Species Act, requiring instead that individualized protection plans be developed for each species. The federal government has removed automatic protections that previously applied to animals when they were added to the threatened species list under the Endangered Species Act. Under the previous policy, a species listed as threatened received the same baseline legal protections as endangered species automatically. Under the new policy, each threatened species will require its own individualized protection plan before those safeguards take effect. Conservation groups and critics argue the change will leave newly listed species vulnerable during the period between listing and the creation of a tailored plan, and warn it could increase the risk of extinctions. Supporters of the change have argued that individualized plans are more appropriate for species with varying circumstances. The Endangered Species Act has been a central tool of US wildlife conservation since 1973 and the policy shift represents a significant change in how it is applied.
