Climate scientists say a strengthening El Niño pattern is expected to intensify through fall and winter, bringing elevated rainfall and potential flooding to portions of the western and southeastern United States, persisting into early spring 2027. Climate models indicate that an El Niño pattern — a periodic warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific — is expected to intensify significantly this fall and winter, with researchers using the term 'super El Niño' to describe its projected strength. The warming sea surface is associated with shifts in atmospheric wind patterns that typically bring above-normal precipitation to the western United States and parts of the southeastern U.S. during winter months. Scientists project the pattern could persist through early spring 2027. Coastal regions of the western U.S. that experienced drought in recent years could see flooding conditions, particularly in low-lying areas and regions where infrastructure has been stressed by wildfire damage. El Niño events of similar strength have historically produced significant damage from flooding and landslides in California and neighboring states. Climate scientists note that background warming from climate change may amplify the effects of strong El Niño episodes beyond historical norms. Federal and state emergency management agencies typically use advance El Niño forecasts to prepare flood response resources.
