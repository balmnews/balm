Trump Administration Removes Fifty-Year Habitat Protections Under the Endangered Species Act

The Trump administration finalized a rule eliminating a longstanding provision of the Endangered Species Act that prohibited harm to the habitats of protected wildlife. The change opens those habitats to logging, mining, and other development for the first time in 50 years. The Trump administration has finalized a significant rollback of the Endangered Species Act, repealing a provision that had been in place for five decades. The rule change removes protections against activities that damage or destroy the habitats of imperiled wildlife, opening those areas to logging, drilling, and mining operations. The provision that was eliminated is considered by ecologists to be one of the most protective elements of the law. Habitat destruction is widely cited as the primary driver of species decline, and environmental advocates note that the existing law has kept roughly 99 percent of listed species from going extinct since its passage in 1973. The administration framed the rollback as removing unnecessary regulatory barriers to economic activity on federal and private lands. Industry groups, including those representing timber, mining, and energy sectors, had long sought changes to what they described as overly broad land-use restrictions. Conservation organizations have indicated legal challenges are likely. The rule change affects all species currently listed under the Endangered Species Act, which includes hundreds of animals and plants found across the United States. The practical effect will depend on how quickly and extensively development interests seek access to newly unprotected habitat areas. Environmental groups argue the change could accelerate population declines for already vulnerable species at a time when biodiversity loss is considered a significant ecological concern.
