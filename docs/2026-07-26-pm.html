India's Education Minister Resigns After Months of Generational Protest Movement

India's education minister resigned following sustained pressure from the Cockroach Janta Party, a protest movement that began as a satirical response to a judicial comment and grew into the most significant public challenge to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government in years. India's education minister resigned after months of pressure from a protest movement known as the Cockroach Janta Party, or CJP — an organization that began as an act of political satire after a judge made a dismissive comment about young people, then expanded into a broad generational movement. The protests represented the most visible public challenge to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government in his twelve years in power. Modi had appeared confident the movement would not substantially threaten his administration even as the protests grew. The CJP announced it was calling off its demonstrations following the minister's departure. Observers noted that the outcome signaled meaningful political pressure from younger Indian voters, and that the government's response to the movement may shape the political landscape ahead.

Former Food and Drug Administration Commissioner Dr. Scott Gottlieb stated publicly that it was not standard operating procedure for Taylor Farms to contact the White House seeking to delay a product recall tied to a significant cyclospora outbreak. Gottlieb made his comments in a television appearance. The recall involved Taylor Farms products and came amid a widespread cyclospora illness investigation. The FDA has not publicly responded to the characterization of events.

ICE Planning Large-Scale Operations Targeting Haitian Migrants Losing Temporary Protected Status

ICE is preparing significant enforcement operations targeting Haitian migrants who are losing Temporary Protected Status following a Supreme Court ruling, with operations expected to begin as soon as this week, according to sources. Immigration and Customs Enforcement is preparing to substantially scale up operations aimed at locating and arresting Haitian migrants whose Temporary Protected Status is expiring following a recent Supreme Court ruling, according to sources with knowledge of the plans. The operations could begin as early as this week. Temporary Protected Status, or TPS, is a form of humanitarian protection granted to nationals of certain countries experiencing ongoing armed conflict, environmental disaster, or other extraordinary conditions. The Supreme Court ruling cleared the way for the administration to terminate TPS for Haitian nationals, a population that has relied on the designation for legal residence in the United States. The planned operations represent one of the larger enforcement actions targeting a specific national group since the current administration took office.

Tadej Pogačar of Slovenia won the 2026 Tour de France, his fifth career title in seven years, joining the small group of riders to win the race five times. The final stage route was shortened due to wildfire activity in southwestern France. Slovenian cyclist Tadej Pogačar claimed victory at the 2026 Tour de France, securing his fifth title in seven years of competing in the race. The win places him alongside a small number of riders who have won the Tour de France five times. His closest rival, Remco Evenepoel of Belgium, finished second. The final stage in Paris was modified for safety reasons, with the route shortened and the iconic climb of the Butte de Montmartre neutralized due to concerns about wet cobblestones, while security and police resources in southwestern France were redirected to assist with active wildfire response in that region. Pogačar stated after the race that he continues to believe he is improving, a remark that drew attention from competitors and cycling observers.
