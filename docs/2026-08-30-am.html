Icelandic voters narrowly rejected a government proposal to reopen EU accession negotiations, with 52.8% voting no in a closely contested referendum. Iceland will not resume European Union membership talks after voters rejected the government's proposal in a national referendum. According to the national broadcaster RUV, 52.8% of voters opposed restarting negotiations, while 47.2% were in favor. The result ends what had been a divisive national debate that split the country between those favoring closer ties with Europe and those prioritizing independence. Iceland applied for EU membership in 2009 but suspended accession talks in 2013. The current government had sought to revive those negotiations amid shifting global conditions. The vote reflects a longstanding tension in Icelandic public opinion between the country's traditional reliance on European trade and its strong preference for maintaining control over its fishing waters and national sovereignty. Iceland is already integrated into the European single market through the European Economic Area, meaning it follows most EU rules without being a formal member. The referendum result effectively closes the door on formal membership talks for the foreseeable future.
