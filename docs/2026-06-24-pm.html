The molecules were identified by the rover's SHERLOC instrument, which uses ultraviolet laser spectroscopy to analyze organic chemistry. Perseverance has been collecting rock cores that are intended for eventual return to Earth, where they can be analyzed with laboratory equipment far more powerful than anything carried on the rover.

Europe Heatwave Breaks Temperature Records in UK and France, Affecting Over 90 Million People

The UK recorded its hottest June day in history at 36.1°C in Hampshire, while France set its all-time temperature record for the second consecutive day, as a heatwave affected more than 90 million people across western Europe. A major heatwave swept across western Europe on Wednesday, breaking national temperature records in the United Kingdom and France and placing more than two-thirds of the European population in conditions above 30°C. The UK recorded 36.1°C in Hampshire, surpassing the previous June record of 35.6°C set in Southampton and eclipsing earlier readings from Surrey and West Sussex during the same afternoon. France recorded its highest temperature ever for the second day in a row, with parts of the country reaching 40°C and red heat alerts issued across 72 of France's 96 mainland departments. The World Health Organization issued guidance during the event, and meteorologists warned that Thursday could bring even higher temperatures in parts of the UK, with forecasts reaching 39°C. Scientists and public health officials noted that high humidity was compounding the heat, causing a 35°C temperature to feel closer to 41°C — a factor that makes heat stress more dangerous for vulnerable populations. Meteorologists attribute the event to a heat dome, a weather system in which high pressure traps warm air over a region, causing temperatures to build over successive days. Europe has been identified as the fastest-warming continent, with local land and sea factors amplifying the global temperature rise caused by greenhouse gas emissions. The event has reignited debate in France over air conditioning, a technology the country has historically resisted for cultural and environmental reasons. The UK's National Health Service had previously been warned that extreme heat events were placing increasing pressure on hospital capacity.
