Swiss voters rejected a referendum proposal to cap the country's population at 10 million, with approximately 54.8% voting against the measure and 45.2% in favor. Turnout was 58.86%. Swiss voters rejected a referendum Sunday that would have capped the country's population at 10 million, a proposal put forward by the Swiss People's Party. Final results showed approximately 54.8% voting against the measure and 45.2% in favor, with turnout at 58.86%. The initiative was framed around concerns over housing affordability, infrastructure pressure, and sustainability following significant population growth — Switzerland's resident population has grown by more than a quarter since 2000, driven largely by immigration. Critics argued the measure would have required drastic reductions in immigration and created significant legal and economic complications, including with Switzerland's bilateral agreements with the European Union. The outcome continues a pattern in which Swiss voters have rejected the most restrictive immigration proposals advanced by the Swiss People's Party, though the party's influence on the country's migration debate remains significant.

A newer approach to editing human embryo DNA, known as base editing, has advanced enough to prompt serious debate among fertility specialists, biotech companies, and bioethicists. Base editing allows precise changes to individual DNA letters without cutting the genome, which proponents argue is safer and more targeted than earlier gene-editing methods. Supporters say the technology offers a path to eliminating inherited genetic diseases before birth. Critics argue that without clear regulatory boundaries, the same tools could be applied to non-medical traits, raising concerns about enhancement and equity. No consensus has emerged among the scientific or policy communities on how and whether clinical applications should proceed.
