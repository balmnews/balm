Measles Officially Returns to Endemic Status in the United States

Federal health authorities have determined that measles has officially returned to endemic circulation in the United States, ending the country's decades-long elimination status. Regaining elimination status could take years. The United States has lost its measles elimination status, with health authorities confirming that the disease is now circulating endemically within the country. The US had maintained elimination status — defined as the absence of continuous year-round transmission — for more than two decades following widespread vaccination. The return of endemic measles reflects a gradual erosion of vaccination coverage in certain communities over recent years, allowing the virus to find sufficient susceptible hosts to sustain ongoing transmission. Public health officials note that regaining elimination status will require a multi-year effort involving strengthened vaccination campaigns, improved access to immunization, and community outreach in areas where vaccine hesitancy has reduced coverage below protective thresholds. Measles remains one of the most contagious diseases known, and even modest declines in vaccination rates can allow it to re-establish sustained transmission. The development has significant implications for unvaccinated individuals, immunocompromised people who cannot receive vaccines, and infants too young to be vaccinated.
