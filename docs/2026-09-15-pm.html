EPA Removes All Emissions Limits on Coal and Gas Power Plants

The Trump administration's Environmental Protection Agency finalized a rule eliminating all emissions limits on coal and gas power plants, effectively removing a central tool for regulating greenhouse gas pollution from the electricity sector under the Clean Air Act. The Environmental Protection Agency under the Trump administration has finalized a rule stripping away all existing limits on greenhouse gas emissions from coal and natural gas power plants, the largest single source of electricity-sector carbon emissions in the United States. The action effectively removes the agency's authority to regulate those emissions under the Clean Air Act as applied to power generation, according to environmental legal experts. The EPA's move comes in the aftermath of what meteorologists described as the hottest summer ever recorded in the contiguous United States. Environmental groups and legal experts characterized the rule as one of the most consequential rollbacks of federal environmental regulation in decades, and litigation is expected. The power generation sector is the subject of longstanding regulatory disputes about how much authority the EPA has under the Clean Air Act to regulate carbon dioxide, which the Supreme Court addressed in its 2022 West Virginia v. EPA decision. The new rule goes further by removing existing standards entirely.
