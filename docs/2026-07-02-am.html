Vatican Excommunicates Society of St. Pius X, Declares It in Schism

The Vatican excommunicated all bishops and priests of the Society of St. Pius X and declared the traditionalist group in schism after it consecrated new bishops without Pope Leo XIV's consent. The Vatican declared the Society of St. Pius X in formal schism on Thursday and excommunicated its bishops and priests, following the group's decision to consecrate new bishops without the authorization of Pope Leo XIV. The announcement was issued by Cardinal Víctor Manuel Fernández, who leads the Holy See's Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith. The Society of St. Pius X, known as the SSPX, is a traditionalist Catholic organization that has operated in tension with mainstream Church authority for decades. It was originally founded in 1970 by Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre, who was himself excommunicated in 1988 after similarly unauthorized episcopal consecrations — a penalty that was later lifted in 2009 as part of reconciliation efforts under Pope Benedict XVI. The new consecrations represent a fresh rupture, and the Vatican's response — full excommunication and a declaration of schism — is its most serious ecclesiastical penalty. The SSPX has an estimated membership running into the tens of thousands globally, including a presence in the United States. A schism means the group is formally separated from communion with Rome. The move is described as the first major ecclesiastical crisis of Pope Leo XIV's papacy.
