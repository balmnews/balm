The United States has officially lost its measles elimination status, according to researchers with direct knowledge of deliberations at the Pan American Health Organization. The US had maintained elimination status — defined as the absence of continuous disease transmission for twelve months or more — since 2000. That status has now been revoked following an extended period of elevated domestic measles cases. Public health officials have attributed the trend to declining vaccination rates in some communities, a pattern that accelerated during the COVID-19 pandemic and has continued in its aftermath. The development is significant because measles is highly contagious, and elimination status serves as a benchmark for a country's immunization infrastructure. The loss of that status places the US alongside countries that have never achieved or have previously lost elimination standing. The Centers for Disease Control has not independently confirmed the PAHO determination as of this reporting.
