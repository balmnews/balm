Germany's AfD Wins Saxony-Anhalt State Election by Wide Margin but Falls Short of Governing Majority

Germany's far-right Alternative for Germany party is projected to win approximately 44 percent of the vote in the Saxony-Anhalt state election — more than double the second-place conservatives at 17 percent. Without a majority, the party cannot form a government on its own, and mainstream parties have maintained a policy of refusing to govern in coalition with the AfD. Exit polls from Sunday's state election in Saxony-Anhalt, an eastern German state, show the Alternative for Germany party, known as the AfD, winning a commanding first place with approximately 44 percent of the vote, far ahead of the second-place conservatives who polled around 17 percent. The result, which the AfD has called historic, would mark the party's strongest performance in any German state election since World War II. However, the AfD fell short of the absolute majority it sought, meaning it cannot form a government without coalition partners. All major mainstream parties in Germany have maintained what is commonly called a firewall — a collective refusal to enter coalition government with the AfD — leaving the party's path to governing power unclear. The AfD's lead candidate in Saxony-Anhalt is Ulrich Siegmund, a 35-year-old who has built a significant following on social media, particularly among younger voters. The result extends a trend of AfD strength in the former East German states and is closely watched across Europe as an indicator of far-right political momentum. The AfD's platform is primarily defined by opposition to immigration and skepticism of climate policy. The outcome has direct relevance for German national politics ahead of federal elections and for European Union cohesion on migration and foreign policy.
