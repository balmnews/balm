NOAA Confirms Onset of El Niño, Predicts Potentially Historic Intensification by Fall

US weather officials confirmed the arrival of an El Niño event, warning it could intensify to historic levels by autumn. Meteorologists say the pattern is likely to amplify heat waves, floods, and other extreme weather already worsened by climate change. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration officially declared the onset of an El Niño event, the periodic warming of Pacific Ocean surface temperatures that reshapes weather patterns worldwide. Scientists project the current event could intensify to the strongest of the century by fall. The UN Secretary-General described the development as an urgent climate warning. El Niño typically increases global average temperatures and is associated with more intense heat waves, heavier rainfall in some regions, and drought in others. For the United States, strong El Niño events historically correlate with wetter winters in the South and Southwest, drier conditions in the Pacific Northwest and northern plains, and heightened hurricane activity in certain basins. The announcement follows record winter temperatures in Antarctica, where temperatures rose above 15 degrees Celsius this month, shattering previous winter heat records for the region and prompting concern among scientists about the pace of climate change. The combination of an El Niño event layered on top of already-elevated baseline temperatures from long-term climate change is expected to make the coming months particularly notable for weather extremes.
