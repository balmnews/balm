Six months into the U.S. military conflict with Iran, officials and analysts say American goals have shifted from initial objectives, and the Strait of Hormuz has become the dominant strategic concern. FDA Approves Daraxonrasib for Pancreatic Cancer, Doubling Patient Survival in Trial

The FDA has approved daraxonrasib, an oral drug for pancreatic cancer that doubled patient survival compared to chemotherapy in a key clinical trial, marking a significant advance for one of the deadliest cancers. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved daraxonrasib, a pill for the treatment of pancreatic cancer. In the pivotal clinical trial that supported approval, patients who received the drug lived twice as long as those who received standard chemotherapy. Pancreatic cancer is among the hardest cancers to treat, with historically poor survival rates and few effective options, making this the most significant drug approval in the disease in many years. The drug works by targeting a genetic mutation common in pancreatic tumors. Researchers and pharmaceutical companies are now testing dozens of similar drugs targeting comparable mutations across other cancers, including lung, colon, and others, raising hopes that the approach could produce advances across multiple cancer types. The approval is expected to trigger increased investment in the drug class.

Shingles Vaccine Linked to Reduced Risk of Heart Attack and Stroke in Study of 70,000 Americans

A study of 70,000 people in the U.S. found that recipients of the Shingrix shingles vaccine had a 12% lower risk of heart attack and stroke compared to those who did not receive it. Researchers analyzing health records of approximately 70,000 people in the United States found that those who received the Shingrix vaccine — the current recommended shingles vaccine — had a 12% lower risk of heart attack and stroke compared to unvaccinated individuals. Scientists said the findings suggest the vaccine could help prevent thousands of deaths each year beyond its primary purpose of protecting against shingles, a painful nerve condition caused by the reactivation of the chickenpox virus. The exact mechanism linking shingles vaccination to cardiovascular benefit is still being studied, but the association held across the dataset. Shingrix is already recommended for adults 50 and older in the United States. The study adds to a growing body of evidence suggesting some vaccines may have broader health benefits than their primary indication.

Meta Settles Teen Safety Lawsuit for $17.1 Billion, Agreeing to New Restrictions on Youth Accounts

Meta has reached a $17.1 billion settlement with U.S. states over harms to teenage users, agreeing to substantial changes to how minors use Instagram and Facebook, including time limits, stronger age checks, and restricted features. Meta has agreed to a $17.1 billion settlement with U.S. states that sued the company over its platforms' effects on young users. As part of the deal, Meta will implement a series of default safety restrictions for users under 18 on Instagram and Facebook. These include a hard daily screen time limit of two hours, a nighttime curfew blocking access between midnight and 6 a.m., strengthened age verification to prevent children under 13 from accessing the platforms, age-appropriate account settings applied by default rather than requiring users to opt in, and the removal of visible like counts. The settlement resolves litigation that drew on research linking heavy social media use among adolescents to increased rates of anxiety, depression, and other mental health concerns. The agreement is considered a legal landmark, and analysts suggest it will pressure competing platforms — including YouTube and TikTok — to adopt similar guardrails, since Meta is expected to push for industry-wide standards. Some critics, including a key expert witness in the case, argued the settlement's terms do not go far enough to meaningfully address the platforms' structural incentives to maximize engagement among young users. Others have noted that the settlement may ultimately cost Meta less than anticipated and could disadvantage rivals who must now navigate the same regulatory scrutiny without the benefit of Meta's negotiated terms. The outcome is widely expected to reshape the regulatory and legal landscape for social media companies in the United States.
