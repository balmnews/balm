US and Iran Agree to 60-Day Roadmap Toward Nuclear Deal; Sanctions Relief and Inspections Announced

Following talks in Switzerland, the US and Iran agreed to a framework for a final nuclear deal within 60 days. Iran will allow UN nuclear inspectors to return, and the US will ease sanctions on Iranian oil exports. The United States and Iran concluded a round of negotiations in Switzerland by agreeing to a roadmap aimed at reaching a final nuclear agreement within 60 days. Vice President JD Vance described the talks as laying a strong foundation, though he emphasized that the framework is a starting point and not a completed deal. Under the terms announced, Iran will permit UN nuclear inspectors to return to the country, the Strait of Hormuz will reopen to normal shipping traffic, and the US will lift sanctions on Iranian oil exports. Long-term independent monitoring of Iran's nuclear program is also part of the framework. The provisions broadly resemble elements of the 2015 nuclear agreement that President Trump withdrew from during his first term in 2018. The deal faces significant unresolved issues. Analysts point to a dispute over billions of dollars in frozen Iranian assets as a potential sticking point. Iran's negotiating delegation in Switzerland was notably large, including its central bank governor and oil officials, which observers interpreted as a signal that Tehran is focused on securing immediate economic relief. Within the United States, the agreement has divided opinion. A CBS News/YouGov poll found that 37 percent of Americans believe the framework benefits Iran more, 22 percent say it favors the US, and 47 percent see it as balanced. Within the Republican Party, some prominent figures have expressed reservations, arguing the deal offers too much to Iran without sufficient guarantees. Former Vice President Mike Pence described it as falling short of what is needed to address the Iranian threat. The framework's direct implications for American readers include potential changes to global oil prices, the reopening of a critical shipping lane, and the broader question of Iran's nuclear trajectory.
