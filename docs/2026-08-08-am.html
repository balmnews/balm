FDA Approves Moderna's mRNA Influenza Vaccine

The FDA approved Moderna's mRNA-based flu vaccine after initially declining to review the application, then reversing course following public pressure. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved Moderna's mRNA influenza vaccine, clearing a new option for flu prevention that uses the same messenger RNA technology platform as Moderna's COVID-19 vaccines. The approval came after an unusual sequence of events: the FDA initially declined to review the application, then reversed that decision following a public response to the initial refusal. The approval adds a second mRNA vaccine type to the U.S. market alongside existing flu shots made through traditional methods. mRNA vaccines can in theory be updated more quickly in response to shifts in circulating flu strains, which proponents argue could improve vaccine effectiveness in years when the traditional manufacturing process does not closely match the dominant strains. Moderna's flu vaccine will enter a market where annual vaccination is already widely recommended by public health authorities. The FDA's initial reluctance to review the application had drawn attention given the broader regulatory environment around vaccine approvals.
