New York state filed a lawsuit against 3M, DuPont, and several other companies, alleging they manufactured and sold consumer goods containing per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, commonly known as PFAS or forever chemicals, while withholding information from the public about the associated health and environmental risks. The lawsuit claims the companies engaged in fraud by concealing what they knew about the dangers of PFAS even as they began phasing the chemicals out of their own products. The state argues this constitutes a public nuisance under New York law. PFAS chemicals are so named because their chemical bonds do not break down naturally in the environment or in the human body. They have been linked in studies to a range of health conditions including certain cancers, immune system effects, and developmental issues. The chemicals were widely used for decades in non-stick cookware, water-resistant clothing, food packaging, and firefighting foam. New York's lawsuit is one of numerous state-level legal actions against PFAS manufacturers in recent years. Several companies, including 3M and DuPont, have previously reached large settlements with other states and municipalities over PFAS contamination of drinking water. The outcome of this suit could affect how companies account for ongoing PFAS-related liability.
