Bank revenue from overdraft fees is increasing following Congress's repeal last year of a federal rule that capped how much banks could charge customers who overdraw their accounts. Banks are collecting increasing revenue from overdraft fees following Congress's elimination last year of a federal rule that had capped those charges. The rule, which had been finalized by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, limited overdraft fees to a set dollar amount per transaction. With the cap removed, banks have returned to setting their own fee levels, and aggregate industry revenue from overdrafts has risen as a result. Overdraft fees are charged when a customer spends more than is available in their account and the bank covers the shortfall. Consumer advocates have long argued these fees fall disproportionately on lower-income account holders who maintain smaller balances. The policy reversal is relevant to any American with a checking account, particularly those who occasionally overdraw.
