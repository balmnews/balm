2026 Atlantic Hurricane Season Sets Record for Slowest Start in Satellite Era

The 2026 Atlantic hurricane season has set a record for the longest period without a storm reaching hurricane-strength winds of 74 mph, at least since reliable satellite records began. El Niño conditions are cited as a contributing factor. As of Saturday, the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season has set a record for the longest stretch since the start of a season without a storm reaching the 74-mile-per-hour sustained wind threshold required to be classified as a hurricane. The record applies to the satellite era, meaning since the mid-twentieth century when consistent observational data became available. Meteorologists attribute the unusually quiet season in part to El Niño conditions, which typically suppress Atlantic hurricane activity by increasing upper-level wind shear that disrupts storm formation and organization. The slow start is notable given that recent years have featured active or above-average Atlantic hurricane seasons, and that the Gulf Coast and Atlantic seaboard states had been preparing for elevated storm activity. However, forecasters note that a slow start does not guarantee a quiet end to the season, which officially runs through November 30, and that El Niño conditions can shift. Residents in hurricane-prone areas are advised to maintain readiness regardless of current season activity.
