Scientists have identified a blood biomarker that may eventually allow doctors to diagnose chronic traumatic encephalopathy, or CTE, in living patients — a development that would be a significant advance in brain injury research. CTE is a degenerative brain condition caused by repeated head trauma, associated most closely with contact sports including American football and rugby. Currently, the disease can only be confirmed through post-mortem examination of brain tissue. The preliminary study found that measuring a protein already associated with Alzheimer's disease showed promise in identifying an advanced form of CTE. Researchers described the finding as a first step and emphasized that the work is early-stage — further research will be needed before any diagnostic test could be developed or validated for clinical use. If confirmed, a blood-based test for CTE would have implications for professional athletes, veterans, and others with histories of repeated head injuries, potentially enabling earlier intervention and treatment.
