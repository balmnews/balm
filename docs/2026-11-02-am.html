UN Report Finds Overshooting 1.5°C Climate Target Would Cause Permanent Damage to Earth's Ice and Snow

A UN report concludes that exceeding the 1.5°C global warming threshold would cause permanent, widespread loss of glaciers, sea ice, and snowpack, challenging the assumption that such damage could be reversed if temperatures are later brought back down. A report from a United Nations body finds that Earth's frozen regions — glaciers, polar ice sheets, seasonal snowpack, and permafrost — face permanent damage if global temperatures overshoot the 1.5°C target set under the Paris Agreement, even temporarily. The report challenges what researchers describe as a persistent myth: the assumption that overshooting temperature targets is acceptable as long as temperatures are eventually brought back down. Scientists behind the report argue that some damage to the cryosphere would be irreversible on human timescales, and that to avoid the most severe outcomes, warming should ultimately be brought back below 1°C. The finding has implications for climate policy, as many current emissions trajectories assume some degree of overshoot before stabilization. Loss of ice and snow at scale affects sea levels, freshwater availability, weather patterns, and ecosystems globally.
