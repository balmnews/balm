US and Iran Sign Preliminary Agreement to End Conflict, Reopen Strait of Hormuz

The US and Iran have signed a 14-point preliminary memorandum of understanding to end their 110-day conflict, including provisions to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, waive certain oil sanctions, and establish a 60-day window for nuclear talks. The deal includes a $300 billion reconstruction framework for Iran. The United States and Iran have signed a preliminary memorandum of understanding to end a conflict that has lasted approximately 110 days, cost thousands of lives, and caused significant disruption to the global economy. The Trump administration released the text of the 14-point agreement, describing it as a major diplomatic achievement. Key terms include the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping, the waiving of certain US oil sanctions on Iran, provisions related to Lebanon, and a 60-day period for further negotiations on Iran's nuclear program. The agreement also references a $300 billion framework for Iran's reconstruction, though President Trump publicly disputed that the United States would directly contribute to that fund. The deal has drawn significant criticism from both Republican and Democratic lawmakers. Several Senate Republicans described it as a major foreign policy failure, arguing the United States made substantial financial and political concessions in exchange for limited Iranian commitments. Democratic critics similarly questioned the terms. Vice President JD Vance pushed back against comparisons to the 2015 Obama-era nuclear agreement, arguing the structure of incentives and penalties differs meaningfully. Separately, three Iranian tankers loaded with crude oil were observed passing the US military blockade line in the Gulf of Oman prior to the agreement being signed, according to ship-tracking data, underscoring the practical pressures that shaped the negotiations. An independent poll found that 64 percent of American voters doubt the new agreement will prevent Iran from eventually developing nuclear weapons — a level of skepticism consistent with public polling at the time of the 2015 deal. The preliminary agreement leaves key details to be resolved in the 60-day follow-on negotiation period, meaning the full terms remain unsettled.
