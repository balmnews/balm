Treasury Department Will Not Enforce Shell Company Disclosure Requirements Under 2021 Law

The Trump administration has announced it will not enforce the reporting requirements of the Corporate Transparency Act of 2021, which required U.S. shell companies to disclose their beneficial owners to combat money laundering. The Treasury Department has announced it will not enforce the beneficial ownership reporting requirements established by the Corporate Transparency Act, a 2021 law designed to reduce the use of anonymous shell companies for money laundering and other financial crimes. Under the law, most U.S. companies were required to disclose the identities of their actual owners to a federal database maintained by the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network. The Trump administration's decision not to enforce those requirements effectively suspends a central mechanism of the law. Critics of the move argue it removes a significant tool for tracking illicit financial flows and that it benefits individuals who use corporate structures to obscure assets. Supporters of the decision argue the law imposed excessive compliance burdens on small businesses. The law had already faced legal challenges and enforcement delays before the administration's announcement.
