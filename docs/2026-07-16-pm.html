Scientists have detected an atmosphere around an Earth-sized exoplanet designated LHS 1140b, located 49 light years from Earth, and say conditions could allow liquid water to exist on its surface. Researchers have confirmed the presence of an atmosphere around LHS 1140b, an Earth-sized planet orbiting a star approximately 49 light years away. The discovery is notable because atmospheres have previously been detected around gas giant exoplanets and so-called super-Earths, but confirming one around a planet closer to Earth's size is considered a significant step in the search for potentially habitable worlds. Scientists say the atmospheric conditions of LHS 1140b could be consistent with the existence of liquid water on its surface, which is considered a key requirement for life as it is understood. The findings add to a growing body of evidence about planets beyond our solar system that may share characteristics with Earth, though direct detection of water or signs of life would require further observation.

Developing El Niño Could Make 2027 the Hottest Year on Record, Scientists Say

Australia's Bureau of Meteorology says the El Niño climate pattern now developing in the Pacific Ocean could become the strongest on record, potentially making 2027 the hottest year in recorded history. Australia's Bureau of Meteorology has warned that the El Niño climate phenomenon currently developing in the Pacific Ocean could intensify into the strongest on record. If that occurs, scientists say 2027 could surpass all previous years as the hottest on record globally. El Niño events are associated with elevated sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific and are linked to widespread effects including droughts, intensified storms, reduced rainfall in some regions, and significantly higher global average temperatures. The current El Niño is being watched by climatologists with increasing concern, given that global baseline temperatures are already elevated compared to historical norms. The Bureau of Meteorology says most Australian capital cities face at least an 80 percent chance of experiencing an unusually warm and dry spring season. The potential for a record-breaking El Niño carries implications for agriculture, water supply, and extreme weather events across multiple continents, including in regions that supply food to global markets.
