SpaceX Rocket Debris Confirmed to Have Struck the Moon; Scientists Study the Impact

A four-ton discarded SpaceX rocket stage struck the moon at roughly 5,400 mph in an unintentional collision. Telescopes captured spectral evidence of sodium and lithium gases released on impact, and scientists say a new lunar crater was likely created. A four-ton upper stage from a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket, which had been drifting in space since launching a pair of lunar landers last year, struck the moon at approximately 5,400 miles per hour in what scientists describe as an unintentional collision. The European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope in Chile captured confirmation of the impact by detecting spectral lines — emissions from sodium and lithium gases ejected by the force of the strike — along with a visible plume. Scientists say a new crater was almost certainly formed, though its size has not yet been confirmed. The rocket stage, roughly the height of a five-story building, was placed on a collision course by a combination of solar radiation pressure and gravitational forces over the course of the year it spent in orbit. The event posed no hazard to Earth. Researchers are now studying the impact data for what it can reveal about the moon's surface composition, and the incident has renewed discussion about the growing volume of uncontrolled space debris in Earth's vicinity.
