The extended talks reflect a persistent gap between countries heavily dependent on fossil fuel revenues and those pushing for stronger commitments to phase them out. No new draft agreement had emerged as of the extended session, and the outcome remained uncertain.

Anthropic Mathematician Claims AI-Assisted Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

A mathematician employed at Anthropic says he used the company's Claude AI to help develop a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, one of the most significant unsolved problems in mathematics. The claim has not yet been independently verified. Levent Alpöge, a Turkish-American mathematician working at AI company Anthropic, posted a note on his personal website claiming he had used the company's Claude AI system to help prove the Riemann hypothesis. The hypothesis, formulated in the 19th century, concerns the distribution of prime numbers and is considered one of the most important and long-standing unsolved problems in mathematics. It is one of the Millennium Prize Problems, for which a correct proof carries a one-million-dollar award. Alpöge's claim has not been independently verified by the broader mathematical community, and peer review of a proof of this complexity typically takes considerable time. The announcement is the second in recent months in which an AI company or someone affiliated with one has claimed to have resolved a major open mathematical problem, raising questions about the role AI tools are playing in formal mathematics. Anthropic has not made an official statement on behalf of the company regarding the claim. The mathematical community is expected to scrutinize the posted work before any consensus on its validity is reached.
