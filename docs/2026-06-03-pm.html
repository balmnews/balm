Studies Find GLP-1 Weight-Loss Drugs May Reduce Cancer Risk by Up to 30%

Multiple studies presented at a major oncology conference suggest that GLP-1 weight-loss drugs used to treat obesity may reduce the risk of developing or dying from cancer by up to 30 percent. A series of studies presented at the world's largest oncology conference indicate that GLP-1 drugs, a class of medications already widely used to treat obesity and diabetes, may have a meaningful effect on cancer risk. Researchers reported that the drugs appear to reduce the likelihood of developing or dying from cancer by approximately 30 percent across multiple cancer types. The findings add to growing evidence that these medications, which work in part by reducing body weight, may have broad health benefits beyond their original indications. Excess body weight is a known risk factor for several common cancers, and reducing it is associated with lower cancer incidence. However, researchers cautioned that the studies are preliminary and that more work is needed to understand the mechanisms involved, which populations benefit most, and whether the cancer risk reduction is driven primarily by weight loss or by other effects of the drugs. The results are being closely watched given the enormous number of people already taking GLP-1 medications globally.
