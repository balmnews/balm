New research finds that housing affordability problems are increasingly affecting middle-income renters in the US, with one in five renters reporting difficulty paying rent in full and on time last year. A new study finds that rental housing affordability challenges — long concentrated among lower-income Americans — are now extending to middle-income households. Researchers found that one in five renters struggled to pay their rent in full and on time in 2025, a share that includes a rising proportion of households earning moderate wages. The findings suggest that elevated rents, which surged during and after the COVID-19 pandemic, have not fully receded even as inflation in other categories has eased. The spread of affordability stress to middle-income earners carries political and economic implications, as this demographic has historically had more financial cushion. Housing economists point to a persistent shortage of rental units in many US markets, combined with higher borrowing costs that have kept many would-be homebuyers in the rental market, as key drivers of the sustained pressure on rents.
