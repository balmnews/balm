A catastrophic flash flood struck the Nepal-Tibet border region on Wednesday, triggered by the collapse of a melting Himalayan glacier that also caused a magnitude 5.2 seismic event. As of Friday, the official death toll was approaching 600, with Nepal's disaster management authority reporting nearly 2,000 people unaccounted for — a figure that more than doubled after approximately 900 workers at hydropower projects were added to the missing count. At least 90 American citizens are among those unaccounted for. Rescue efforts have been complicated by the risk of additional flooding, with experts warning that unstable conditions in the mountain region could produce further events. In Tibet, information has been significantly harder to obtain due to restricted access to the region, meaning the full scope of casualties there remains unclear. Geologists note that dozens of similar glacial lakes in the Himalayas pose comparable risks, and that the frequency and scale of glacial lake outburst floods is expected to increase as warming continues.
