French health authorities described the risk to the broader public as low. France's health ministry confirmed the country's first Ebola case in a doctor who had recently traveled to the Democratic Republic of Congo, where a fast-expanding outbreak of the Bundibugyo strain of the virus is ongoing. Authorities said the risk to the general population in France was low, given that Ebola spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids rather than through airborne transmission. The Bundibugyo strain involved in the current outbreak is the subject of scientific investigation into its animal reservoir — the species in which the virus persists between human outbreaks — which has not yet been identified. Researchers note that the current outbreak may have a lower death rate than previous Ebola outbreaks, which carries a complicating implication: milder symptoms could make it harder to detect cases and control the spread of disease. Key factors affecting the trajectory of the outbreak include the pace of contact tracing, which is reportedly improving, and the availability of treatments, which may still be months away. The outbreak's spread to a high-income country with robust healthcare infrastructure is likely to increase international attention and resources directed at containing the disease in Central Africa.

NASA's Perseverance Rover Detects Complex Carbon Molecules in Martian Rocks

NASA's Perseverance rover has identified complex organic carbon molecules in Martian mudstone samples that scientists have already flagged as candidates for bearing signs of ancient microbial life. NASA's Perseverance rover has detected complex carbon molecules in rock samples from the Bright Angel outcrop on Mars, a site already under scientific scrutiny for its potential to preserve biosignatures — indicators of past biological activity. The rover's onboard instrument, called SHERLOC, identified the organic carbon in mudstone formations. Organic carbon molecules are not themselves proof of life; they can be produced through non-biological geological and chemical processes. However, their presence in rocks that are considered geologically promising for preservation of ancient microbial life adds to the scientific case for continued investigation. The samples are among those targeted for eventual return to Earth, where they could be analyzed with laboratory instruments far more powerful than those available on the rover.
