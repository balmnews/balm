US and Coalition Forces Complete Withdrawal from Iraq, Ending Anti-ISIS Mission

The US military completed its withdrawal from Iraq on Wednesday, closing out Operation Inherent Resolve and relocating its coalition headquarters to Jordan. The departure, agreed in 2024, fulfills a longstanding Iraqi government demand. The United States military has completed its withdrawal from Iraq, ending a mission that began as a counter-ISIS campaign and marking the close of a continuous American military presence in the country that stretches back to the 2003 invasion. The final forces departed Erbil Air Base on Wednesday, and the coalition's operational headquarters has been relocated to Jordan. The withdrawal was agreed upon in 2024 as part of negotiations between Washington and Baghdad, and its completion fulfills a demand the Iraqi government had pursued for years as a matter of sovereignty. British forces were also among the last coalition troops to depart. The Iraqi government described the end of the mission as a sign of the country's growing strength and independence. Reactions within Iraq were mixed: some Iraqis welcomed the end of a foreign military presence they associate with years of instability and conflict; others expressed concern about what follows. Security analysts and officials note two primary risks going forward. Iranian-backed Shia militias, which have long operated with significant influence in Iraq, may seek to expand their footprint with American forces no longer present as a counterweight. Separately, the Islamic State, which has shown signs of regrouping in recent years, could find more room to maneuver. Iraq's own security forces are now responsible for holding those threats in check without direct coalition support. The US military noted that it retains a presence in the broader region and that the mission's headquarters will continue operations from Jordan.

Underwater 'Kelvin Wave' Approaching California Coast, Expected to Raise Sea Levels Temporarily

A large subsurface band of warm water known as a Kelvin wave is approaching Southern California and is expected to temporarily raise coastal sea levels by up to half a foot, increasing risks from high tides and swells. The wave is expected to move north toward the San Francisco Bay Area and Alaska. Coastal communities across California are preparing for the arrival of what oceanographers call a Kelvin wave — a large, deep band of warm ocean water moving eastward from the central Pacific and traveling along the ocean floor toward the coast. When such waves reach shore, they cause a temporary but significant rise in local sea levels, in this case projected at roughly half a foot above normal along Southern California. That rise amplifies the effects of tides and storm swells, increasing the risk of coastal flooding and erosion. The wave is expected to reach Southern California imminently before moving northward toward the San Francisco Bay Area and eventually Alaska. Coastal emergency managers are monitoring conditions and in some areas preparing protective measures. Kelvin waves are naturally occurring oceanographic phenomena and have previously been associated with elevated flooding risk along the Pacific coast.
