Trump Administration Proposes Major Changes to US Census Methodology

The Trump administration has proposed census changes that would eliminate questions about race and exclude millions of US residents from population counts used to draw congressional voting maps, raising constitutional questions under the 14th Amendment. The Trump administration has put forward a set of proposed changes to how the US Census is conducted that would fundamentally alter both what the census measures and who it counts. The proposals would remove questions about race from the census and would exclude millions of US residents — including non-citizens — from the population count used to apportion congressional seats and draw electoral maps. The exclusion proposal is directly contested by the 14th Amendment, which specifies that congressional apportionment must be based on the 'whole number of persons in each state,' language that courts have historically interpreted to include all residents regardless of citizenship status. Legal challenges would be expected if the proposals advance. The practical implications are significant. States with large non-citizen populations — including California, Texas, New York, and Florida — would lose congressional representation under an exclusion-based count, shifting political power toward states with smaller non-citizen populations. The changes to racial categorizations would also affect a wide range of federal programs, civil rights enforcement, and public health research that rely on census demographic data. The proposal represents one of the most consequential potential changes to the census in decades and would affect the distribution of political power in the US for the decade following the next enumeration.

OpenAI says its artificial intelligence system has produced a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem, one of seven Millennium Prize Problems in mathematics that has remained unsolved for nearly a century. The claim has not yet been independently verified. OpenAI announced that its artificial intelligence system has produced what the company describes as a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem, one of seven Millennium Prize Problems — a set of major unsolved questions in mathematics identified by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000. Each carries a $1 million prize and represents a frontier challenge in the field. The Navier-Stokes equations describe the motion of fluids and have resisted a complete mathematical proof for close to a century. The company said the effort involved significant investment and used AI-led mathematical reasoning. If verified, the result would represent the first Millennium Prize Problem to be solved and would mark a significant milestone both for mathematics and for the practical capabilities of AI systems in formal reasoning. Independent verification by the mathematics community has not yet been completed, and experts have noted that extraordinary claims of this kind require rigorous peer review before they can be accepted. The announcement nonetheless reflects a broader transformation in how AI tools are being applied to formal mathematics — automating proof-checking, suggesting novel approaches, and working through combinatorial spaces that would be intractable for human researchers alone. The development is being watched closely by both mathematicians and the broader scientific community.
