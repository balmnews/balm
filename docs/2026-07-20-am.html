Andy Burnham Becomes UK Prime Minister, Succeeding Keir Starmer

Andy Burnham was appointed the United Kingdom's prime minister by King Charles on Sunday, succeeding Keir Starmer, who resigned. Burnham, a Labour Party politician and former Mayor of Greater Manchester, is the first openly Roman Catholic prime minister in British history. Andy Burnham became the United Kingdom's 57th prime minister on Sunday after being appointed by King Charles at Buckingham Palace, following the resignation of Keir Starmer. Both men are members of the center-left Labour Party, meaning the transition did not require a general election — a feature of the UK's parliamentary system, in which the prime minister is the leader of the party holding a parliamentary majority, not a directly elected position. Burnham, who served as Mayor of Greater Manchester for nearly a decade, is the first openly Roman Catholic prime minister in British history, though he has described his faith as a cultural identity rather than a fervent religious practice. In his first speech outside 10 Downing Street, Burnham described his government as a potential turning point for the country, pledging to end rough sleeping, make life more affordable, and move faster than his predecessor on economic reform. He also signaled a desire to redistribute political power away from London to the regions, a priority rooted in his decade running Greater Manchester. Business groups, including the Confederation of British Industry, called for his economic ambitions to be matched with concrete support for business investment. Burnham's past public criticism of former U.S. President Donald Trump has been noted by some observers as a potential early source of friction with the current Washington administration, though no formal diplomatic exchanges have been reported. His appointment has no direct impact on the US-UK special relationship in the immediate term, but American readers with business, travel, or policy interests in the UK will want to track his economic and foreign policy priorities as they develop.

EPA Withdraws Proposed Rules on PFAS-Contaminated Sewage Sludge Used as Fertilizer

The Environmental Protection Agency has withdrawn a proposed rule that would have regulated PFAS-contaminated sewage sludge spread on farmland as fertilizer, discarding prior scientific research and concluding the practice does not pose a significant public health threat. The Environmental Protection Agency under the Trump administration has withdrawn a proposed regulation that would have set limits on the use of sewage sludge contaminated with PFAS — a class of synthetic chemicals sometimes called "forever chemicals" — as agricultural fertilizer. PFAS compounds are associated with a range of health conditions and do not break down easily in the environment. The practice of spreading sewage sludge on farmland is widespread in the United States and has been the subject of litigation and regulatory discussion for years, after cases emerged in which farmers, livestock, and local water supplies showed evidence of PFAS contamination. The EPA's decision also involved setting aside scientific research conducted under the prior administration. The agency stated in its determination that the practice does not represent a significant threat to public health. The decision is likely to face legal challenge from environmental groups and affected farmers.
