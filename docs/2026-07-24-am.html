Study Links Early PFAS Exposure in Children to Higher Rates of Gut Inflammation

New research finds that prenatal and early childhood exposure to mixtures of PFAS chemicals is associated with elevated intestinal inflammation in children, which can contribute to conditions including Crohn's disease and colorectal cancer. Researchers have published findings suggesting that prenatal and early life exposure to mixtures of PFAS — a class of synthetic chemicals found in many consumer products and water supplies — is probably associated with higher levels of intestinal inflammation in children. Elevated intestinal inflammation is a known precursor to conditions including Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, and colorectal cancer. The study is described as the first of its kind to examine the relationship between PFAS exposure and gut inflammation specifically in children. PFAS compounds, sometimes called forever chemicals because they do not break down naturally in the environment or the human body, have already been linked in prior research to a range of other health outcomes including certain cancers, immune disruption, and hormonal effects. The new findings add to the body of evidence about the potential harms of early-life chemical exposure.
