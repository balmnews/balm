European Gas Storage Hits 13-Year Low Ahead of Winter, Raising Supply Concerns

European Union gas storage stands at 63% capacity in late August — well below the recent average of 80% for this time of year — the lowest level in 13 years, raising concerns among energy traders about winter supply adequacy. European gas storage entered the final week of August at 63% capacity, significantly below the approximately 80% average for late August recorded in recent years and the lowest level seen in 13 years. Energy traders and analysts have described the shortfall as triggering concern about whether Europe will have sufficient reserves to meet winter heating demand. The gap relative to historical norms is substantial enough that some market participants are treating it as a potential supply risk rather than a manageable variance. The situation has broader relevance for the US, as European demand for liquefied natural gas — a significant portion of which is supplied by American exporters — could rise sharply if the storage deficit persists into the colder months.

Federal Judge Rules Government Cannot Deport Students Solely for Criticizing Israel

A federal judge ruled that the Trump administration cannot deport international students based on their criticism of Israel, delivering a First Amendment victory to Stanford University's student newspaper. A federal judge ruled Friday that the Trump administration lacks the authority to deport international students solely on the basis of their criticism of Israeli government policy. The ruling, by Judge Noël Wise, was framed as a First Amendment protection and came in a case involving Stanford University's student newspaper, which had argued that international students on its campus were self-censoring out of fear of deportation. The decision limits a key element of the administration's approach to campus protests over the Israel-Gaza conflict, which had included threatening the immigration status of students who participated in demonstrations or made public statements critical of Israel. The ruling does not bar deportation on other grounds, but establishes that political speech alone cannot serve as the basis for removal proceedings against international students.

British Political Commentator Milo Yiannopoulos Deported from US After Immigration Violation

British commentator Milo Yiannopoulos was detained by Immigration and Customs Enforcement in New Orleans and deported to the United Kingdom, after authorities said he had remained in the country past the expiration of his legal authorization. British political commentator Milo Yiannopoulos was detained by US Immigration and Customs Enforcement in New Orleans and subsequently deported to the United Kingdom. The Department of Homeland Security stated that Yiannopoulos had entered the US legally in May 2019 but remained in the country after his authorization expired, and that a deportation order was already in place. ICE described him as an 'illegal alien.' Yiannopoulos is a British citizen who had been a prominent supporter of stricter US immigration enforcement and of President Trump's immigration policies. Multiple sources confirmed his removal from the country on Friday. The deportation attracted attention given the apparent irony of an outspoken immigration enforcement advocate being subject to that same enforcement apparatus.

Two Frankfurt Airport Workers Die from Malaria Likely Contracted via Infected Mosquito on Aircraft

Two workers at Frankfurt Airport in Germany died from malaria this summer in cases health officials believe were caused by mosquitoes that arrived on incoming aircraft. Four other airport employees who were infected recovered. German health officials and Frankfurt Airport's operator confirmed that six airport employees contracted malaria this summer, with two of those cases proving fatal. Health authorities believe the infections were caused by mosquitoes that traveled on board aircraft arriving from malaria-endemic regions — a phenomenon sometimes called 'airport malaria.' The four other infected workers recovered. Malaria is not endemic to Germany, making locally acquired infections rare and typically linked to travel or, in these cases, proximity to aircraft. The deaths have prompted public health attention to the risk posed by infected insects transported on international flights.
