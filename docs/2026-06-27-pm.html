Great Britain's National Energy System Operator issued warnings twice in one week asking electricity generators to supply additional power as demand surged. Scientists described the event as among the most severe and geographically wide heatwaves ever recorded in Europe, and meteorologists warned that above-average temperatures are likely to persist through the summer. The heatwave is forecast to shift eastward, keeping large portions of eastern Europe under extreme heat through the coming days. Public health officials raised particular concern about infants and the elderly, and hospitals in several countries reported a rise in heat-related admissions.

Congo Ebola Outbreak Could Become Largest on Record, Africa CDC Warns

Africa's CDC has warned that the current Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo — caused by the Bundibugyo strain — could surpass all previous outbreaks in scale, as contact tracing falls behind and the virus's animal source remains unidentified. Africa's Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has issued a warning that the ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has the potential to become the deadliest and largest ever recorded. The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo strain of the virus, which scientists believe originates in an animal reservoir that has not yet been identified — a gap that complicates efforts to prevent future spillovers into human populations. Public health officials reported that the majority of people testing positive for Ebola were not already on health workers' contact-tracing lists, indicating that transmission chains are outpacing surveillance efforts. Frontline workers conducting safe burials — a critical step in stopping the spread of the disease, since contact with the deceased is a major transmission route — are facing not only the risk of infection but also hostility from communities frightened by the outbreak. Volunteers have in some cases been turned away or confronted by residents. Previous Ebola outbreaks have been concentrated and controlled, but the scale and geographic spread of this event is raising concern among international health experts. The United States has historical involvement in funding and coordinating global Ebola response efforts, and a worst-case outbreak of this scale would carry potential implications for international travel, global health funding, and US health security preparedness.
