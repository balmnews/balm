White House Moves to Allow Private Companies to Hack Foreign Cybercriminals

A White House memorandum proposes giving private US companies the legal ability to hack or spy on certain foreign cybercriminals, marking a significant shift in how offensive cyber operations could be conducted. The Trump administration issued a White House memorandum outlining a proposal to authorize private American companies to conduct offensive cyber operations — including hacking and surveillance — against certain foreign cybercriminal actors. The memorandum was described as thin on operational details, but represents a notable policy direction that would blur the line between government-authorized cyber action and private sector activity. Currently, hacking of any kind by private entities is broadly restricted under US law regardless of the target's location or activity. Legal experts and cybersecurity analysts noted significant questions remain about oversight, liability, and the risk of escalation if private hacking operations go wrong or are misattributed. The policy would require additional legal framework to take effect. The administration has framed the proposal as a tool to disrupt ransomware and other foreign-based cybercriminal networks that have caused significant harm to American businesses and infrastructure.

Hurricane Lala Approaches Hawaii's Big Island, First Potential Direct Hit in 34 Years

Hurricane Lala, a Category 1 storm with sustained winds of 75 mph, is bearing down on Hawaii's Big Island and could deliver the island's first direct hurricane hit in 34 years, with forecasts of up to 25 inches of rain. Tropical Storm Lala strengthened into a Category 1 hurricane and was approaching Hawaii's Big Island, with the National Hurricane Center reporting maximum sustained winds of 75 miles per hour. If it makes direct landfall, it would be the first direct hurricane strike on Hawaii in 34 years. In a worst-case scenario, forecasters said Lala could dump up to 25 inches, or about 63 centimeters, of rain on the Big Island, raising concerns about flash flooding and dangerous surf. Residents and visitors on the Big Island were advised to monitor official guidance from the National Weather Service and local emergency management authorities and prepare accordingly. Hawaii's geography and infrastructure make direct hurricane landfalls relatively rare, though the islands are periodically affected by tropical systems passing nearby.
