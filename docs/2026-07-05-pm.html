California has enacted a law that standardizes the expiration date labels on food products, addressing a longstanding source of consumer confusion that has contributed to significant food waste. Currently, food products carry a variety of date labels — including 'sell by,' 'best by,' 'use by,' and others — that have no uniform legal meaning and vary by manufacturer. Many consumers discard food based on these dates even when it remains safe to eat, contributing to what advocates say amounts to substantial unnecessary waste. The new California law establishes consistent terminology to distinguish between food safety dates and quality dates, helping consumers make better-informed decisions about whether food is safe to consume. Because California is the most populous US state and a major market, its labeling standards have historically influenced manufacturer practices nationwide.
