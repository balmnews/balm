Keith Thomas of Massapequa, New York, who was paralyzed from the chest down in a swimming accident six years ago, has regained the ability to feed himself and drink from a cup through a brain implant that bypasses his spinal cord injury. Thomas enrolled in a clinical trial of a neural bypass system that reads signals from his brain and routes them around the damaged spinal cord to restore arm and hand function. Before the trial, he could not lift his arms off his wheelchair. The development represents a meaningful advance in neural interface technology, which has been an active area of research for treating paralysis caused by spinal cord injuries. The technique does not repair the underlying injury but creates an alternative signal pathway that enables voluntary movement.

Scientists have identified an atmosphere around LHS 1140b, an Earth-sized planet located 49 light years from our solar system.
