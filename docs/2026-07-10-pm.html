Trump Administration Removes 50-Year Habitat Protections Under Endangered Species Act

The Trump administration finalized a rule eliminating a core provision of the Endangered Species Act that had protected wildlife habitats from development, logging, and mining for 50 years. The Trump administration finalized a rule on Friday that removes a long-standing interpretation of the Endangered Species Act that prohibited activities causing indirect harm to protected species, such as habitat destruction. For five decades, the law had been understood to protect not only individual animals but also the ecosystems they depend on, which conservationists credit with keeping 99 percent of listed species from going extinct. The new rule narrows that protection, opening habitats of imperiled wildlife to development, logging, mining, and other uses even if those activities threaten the species' survival. Habitat destruction is considered the leading driver of species loss globally. Environmental groups condemned the change, while industry groups and some state governments had long sought to narrow what they described as overly broad restrictions on land use. The rule is expected to face legal challenges. The rollback could affect hundreds of species listed under the act, with implications for land management across large portions of the American West and other regions.

New York State has filed a lawsuit against 3M, DuPont, and several other chemical manufacturers, alleging the companies caused a public nuisance by selling products containing per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — commonly known as PFAS or 'forever chemicals' — while concealing known risks to human health and the environment. The state's lawsuit alleges the companies withheld information from the public about the harms of these chemicals even as they began internally phasing them out. The European Commission issued formal charges against Meta, alleging that features like infinite scrolling and autoplay on Facebook and Instagram violate the EU's Digital Services Act by encouraging addictive use. The case is being closely watched by regulators and tech companies globally, as it could set precedents for how platform design is regulated in other jurisdictions. American users of Facebook and Instagram would not be directly affected by an EU ruling, but any design changes Meta makes for European compliance could influence its products worldwide.
