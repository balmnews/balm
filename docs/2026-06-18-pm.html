The United States has lifted its naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, allowing commercial shipping to resume through the strategic waterway for the first time since the US-Iran conflict began. The move followed the signing of a memorandum of understanding between President Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, with Pakistan's prime minister also signing as a party. Under the preliminary terms, the US is providing immediate relief on some oil sanctions, while key nuclear questions are deferred to the next phase of talks. Iran's parliament speaker, a key negotiator, separately warned that Tehran would not honor its commitments if Washington failed to uphold its side. Israel, which was not party to the agreement, has continued military operations in Lebanon, and Vice President Vance issued a pointed warning to Israeli critics of the deal not to alienate the United States. European governments and other international observers are watching closely to see whether the 60-day framework produces a durable settlement. The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil supply, and its reopening is expected to have immediate effects on global energy markets. Analysts differ on the durability of the arrangement, with some noting that the Iranian regime has survived the conflict and, in some respects, emerged with greater internal cohesion.

FDA Advisory Panel Backs First mRNA-Based Influenza Vaccine for Older Adults

An FDA advisory panel has recommended approval of Moderna's mFlusiva, the first influenza vaccine built on mRNA technology, intended for adults 65 and older ahead of the coming flu season. A Food and Drug Administration advisory panel has voted to back the approval of Moderna's mFlusiva, an influenza vaccine developed using the same messenger RNA technology that underpinned the COVID-19 vaccines. If the FDA follows the panel's recommendation, as it typically does, mFlusiva would become the first mRNA-based flu vaccine authorized in the United States. Moderna is seeking approval specifically for adults 65 and older, the age group most vulnerable to severe influenza outcomes. Traditional flu vaccines are produced using egg-based or cell-based manufacturing processes, which can be slower to update as circulating strains evolve. mRNA technology allows for faster reformulation, a potential advantage in years when the flu strain composition changes rapidly. The FDA will make a final authorization decision separately from the panel's vote. If authorized, the vaccine would be available ahead of the coming winter flu season.
