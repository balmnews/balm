Research Points to Possible Blood Test for CTE Diagnosis in Living Patients

New research identifies an unusual protein that may allow clinicians to diagnose chronic traumatic encephalopathy through a blood test in living patients — a capability that does not currently exist, as CTE can only be confirmed after death. Two new studies suggest that an unusual protein may serve as a detectable biomarker for chronic traumatic encephalopathy, or CTE, in living patients. Currently, CTE — a progressive brain disease linked to repeated head trauma — can only be definitively diagnosed through examination of brain tissue after death. A blood test capable of detecting the condition in living individuals would represent a fundamental shift in how the disease is studied and potentially managed. The condition disproportionately affects athletes in contact sports and military veterans who have experienced repeated concussions or blast exposure. Researchers cautioned that additional work is needed to validate the findings before any clinical test could be developed.
