Conservation organizations and scientists warn that the rollback could accelerate extinction risks for more than 400 plant and animal species currently listed as endangered or threatened that depend on intact old-growth forest habitat. Supporters of the rollback argue that it restores access for resource development and benefits rural economies. Critics say the loss of protection for large contiguous forest areas will have long-term consequences for biodiversity, water quality, and carbon storage. The decision is subject to legal challenge.
