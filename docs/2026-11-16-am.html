US Closes Remote-Access Loophole on Advanced AI Chip Exports to China

The Trump administration issued new rules blocking Chinese companies from accessing advanced American AI chips and servers remotely, closing a gap that had allowed indirect access despite restrictions on physical sales to China. The Trump administration has issued new regulations that close a loophole in existing semiconductor export controls, preventing Chinese companies from accessing advanced US AI chips and computing servers through remote means — such as cloud-based services — even when direct physical sales to China are prohibited. The previous rules restricted the export of physical chips but did not address scenarios where a Chinese firm could rent computing time on American-made hardware hosted abroad. The new rules extend restrictions to cover that indirect access. The move follows the resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang amid controversy that included allegations of a relationship with a person linked to a Chinese company, which heightened congressional and executive scrutiny of technology transfer risks. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, whose handling of Nvidia-related chip export policy has drawn attention, is traveling with President Trump to the Shenzhen summit. The rule change is part of a broader US effort to maintain a lead in AI development by limiting China's access to the hardware that underpins large AI models.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has confirmed six deaths linked to measles in the United States in 2026, according to data published on the agency's website. Measles is one of the most contagious infectious diseases known, and its return as a cause of death in the United States marks a significant public health development. The CDC confirmation of six fatalities represents a threshold not seen in many years and underscores concerns among epidemiologists about the consequences of reduced immunization coverage.
