Solar power generated more U.S. electricity than coal in May 2026, accounting for 12.8% of national electricity supply, according to data released by the global energy research organization Ember. This marks the first time solar has surpassed coal in monthly U.S. electricity generation. The development is notable in part because it occurs against the backdrop of the current administration's policy push to expand coal production and reduce regulatory constraints on the fossil fuel industry. Solar capacity has continued to grow rapidly, driven by falling installation costs and existing state-level and utility-level commitments that largely pre-date current federal policy. Coal's share of the electricity mix has declined steadily over the past decade as natural gas and renewables have grown. Energy analysts note that May's figures reflect peak solar production months and that coal may still exceed solar in individual months during lower-sunlight periods, but the broader trend line is expected to continue favoring solar. The shift has implications for U.S. carbon emissions, electricity pricing, and the long-term structure of the energy sector.

Federal Reports Find Medicare Advantage Plans Frequently Deny Short-Term Nursing and Rehabilitation Care

Two reports by U.S. government investigators found that Medicare Advantage plans regularly reject requests for short-term nursing home stays and inpatient rehabilitation services, raising questions about access to care for elderly Americans enrolled in private Medicare plans. Two analyses by U.S. government investigators have found that Medicare Advantage plans — the private insurance alternative to traditional Medicare — frequently deny coverage for short-term nursing home care and inpatient rehabilitation services. Medicare Advantage now covers more than half of all Medicare beneficiaries in the United States, making the findings directly relevant to tens of millions of older Americans. Unlike traditional Medicare, which is administered directly by the federal government, Medicare Advantage plans are run by private insurers who receive a fixed payment per enrollee and retain the difference between that payment and actual care costs, creating financial incentives to limit utilization. The reports found that plans were quick to reject requests for post-acute care — the type of skilled nursing or physical rehabilitation that patients often need after a hospitalization for surgery, stroke, or other serious illness. Denials can result in patients being discharged before they have recovered adequately or being required to pay out of pocket for care that would otherwise be covered. The findings add to a body of scrutiny over the Medicare Advantage program from both government investigators and patient advocacy groups. Congressional attention to the program has grown in recent years, and the reports are likely to factor into ongoing legislative discussions about Medicare reform.

A systematic review and a separate Australian brain study published in 2026 found that diets high in ultra-processed foods are associated with a 37% higher risk of dementia and reduced gray matter volume. Two newly published research studies have strengthened the evidence connecting high consumption of ultra-processed foods to cognitive decline and structural brain changes. Gray matter is involved in memory, decision-making, and sensory processing. The research adds to a growing body of evidence suggesting that diet quality has significant implications for long-term brain health, independent of other lifestyle factors. Researchers noted that ultra-processed foods now make up a substantial proportion of caloric intake in many countries, including the United States, making the public health implications potentially significant. The studies do not establish definitive causation and further research is needed, but the consistency of findings across multiple study types has led nutritional scientists to treat the relationship as increasingly robust.

A new approach to editing the genetic material of human embryos, known as base editing, is generating significant debate within the scientific and bioethical communities. El Niño Officially Forms in Pacific; Scientists Warn of Potential Record-Strength Extreme Weather

U.S. scientists have confirmed the formation of an El Niño event in the Pacific Ocean. Meteorologists say it may rival or exceed the record 1997 El Niño in strength, raising the prospect of significant weather disruption including droughts, floods, and higher global temperatures. U.S. scientists have officially declared the onset of an El Niño event, a natural climate pattern in which warmer-than-average sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific Ocean alter weather patterns around the world. Meteorologists say the current El Niño is forming in an already warmed-up Pacific and is expected to grow to historic strength, potentially rivaling or exceeding the record event recorded in 1997, which caused widespread droughts, flooding, and agricultural disruption globally. El Niño events typically influence U.S. weather by shifting precipitation patterns, often bringing wetter conditions to the southern states and drier conditions to the Pacific Northwest and parts of the Midwest. They are also associated with more active Atlantic hurricane seasons in some years, though effects vary. Scientists note that because this El Niño is developing on top of underlying warming from climate change, its temperature and weather impacts are likely to be more pronounced than historical averages would suggest. No specific predictions for individual storm events have been made, but forecasters are urging preparation for elevated risk of extreme conditions into late 2026 and early 2027.
