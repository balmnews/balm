NOAA Confirms El Niño Has Formed, with Potential to Reach Historic Intensity This Fall

US government forecasters confirmed that El Niño has officially formed in the Pacific Ocean and could intensify to the strongest levels of the century by fall, according to NOAA. The climate pattern is associated with disrupted weather across the globe, including more intense storms, droughts, and heat in various regions. The US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration confirmed on Thursday that an El Niño climate pattern has officially formed in the Pacific Ocean. Forecasters said the event could intensify to historic levels by fall, with some scientists describing it as potentially the strongest El Niño of the current century. El Niño is a periodic warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific that reshapes weather patterns globally. Its effects vary by region but typically include increased rainfall and flooding in some areas, drought in others, and a general tendency to push global average temperatures higher. For the United States, strong El Niño events are historically associated with wetter winters across the southern tier, drier conditions in the Pacific Northwest, and a more active hurricane season in the Pacific while reducing Atlantic hurricane activity. The UN Secretary-General described the development as an urgent climate warning. Forecasters had anticipated El Niño's arrival since earlier this year; the official confirmation allows for more refined regional forecasts in the months ahead.
