Houthi Forces Seize Red Sea Island and Port, Threatening Major Oil Shipping Routes

Houthi rebels captured Perim island in the Bab al-Mandab Strait and the port of Mocha, tightening their grip on a critical Red Sea chokepoint. Saudi Arabia launched airstrikes in response, and Brent crude surpassed $106 per barrel. Houthi forces in Yemen have seized Perim island, a strategically significant island at the entrance to the Bab al-Mandab Strait, along with the port of Mocha along Yemen's western coast. The Bab al-Mandab Strait is one of the world's most critical shipping chokepoints, through which a substantial share of global oil exports and commercial cargo passes between the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden. The seizure gives the Houthis direct leverage over tanker traffic navigating the southern reaches of the Red Sea. Saudi Arabia responded with airstrikes on Houthi-held positions following the territorial advance. Separately, Saudi Arabia announced it had shut down a major oil pipeline after a drone attack launched from Iraq, compounding disruptions to the kingdom's export capacity. Saudi officials reportedly urged the United States to consider direct military action against the Houthis. Oil markets responded sharply, with Brent crude prices rising past $106 per barrel. Shipping operators in the region were already navigating elevated risk, and the new territorial gains have deepened uncertainty over safe transit through both the Red Sea and the broader Persian Gulf region. Accounts of the precise extent of Houthi territorial control differ across sources, but multiple reports confirm the seizure of Perim island and advances toward the strait. The developments have direct implications for American consumers through energy prices, and for the US military and diplomatic posture in the Middle East.

The lawsuits centered on pollution tied to Chemours' Fayetteville Works plant and related facilities.
