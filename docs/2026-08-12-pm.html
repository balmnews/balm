Treasury Department Will Not Enforce Shell Company Disclosure Requirements

The Trump administration has announced it will not enforce reporting requirements under the 2021 Corporate Transparency Act, which required US shell companies to disclose their true owners as a measure against money laundering. The Treasury Department has said it will not enforce the beneficial ownership reporting requirements established by the Corporate Transparency Act, a 2021 law designed to combat money laundering and financial crime by requiring shell companies to identify their real owners. The law was passed with bipartisan support and was intended to close a long-standing gap in US financial oversight, as anonymous shell companies have frequently been used to hide assets and launder money. The administration's decision not to enforce the law effectively suspends its practical effect without formally repealing it. Critics of the move argue it weakens anti-corruption and anti-money laundering protections. The administration has not detailed what, if any, alternative oversight framework it intends to implement.

Major Gulf oil-exporting nations are investing heavily in infrastructure designed to move crude oil to market without passing through the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway between Iran and the Arabian Peninsula through which roughly a fifth of global oil supply normally flows. The investments include expanded pipelines and export terminals that bypass the strait entirely. The push reflects a reassessment of risk among Gulf producers following the US-Iran conflict, which demonstrated how quickly the strait could become a chokepoint. Even as diplomatic efforts toward a ceasefire continue, exporters say they no longer consider exclusive reliance on the Hormuz route acceptable. For American consumers and the global economy, reduced dependence on the strait could help stabilize oil prices during future periods of Middle East tension. The buildout represents one of the most significant shifts in Gulf energy infrastructure in decades and could reshape global oil supply chains over the coming years.
