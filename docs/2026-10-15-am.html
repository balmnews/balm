Researchers Say Online Ads Were Used to Deliver Spyware Without User Clicks

Researchers at the University of Toronto's Citizen Lab say an Israeli advertising agency served online ads that allowed spyware to be installed on targeted devices without any user interaction. Researchers at Citizen Lab, a digital investigations unit at the University of Toronto, published findings alleging that an Israeli advertising company served millions of online advertisements that functioned as delivery vehicles for so-called zero-click spyware — malicious software that can be installed on a device without the user clicking anything or taking any action. The researchers said they believed dozens of devices had been compromised. Zero-click exploits are considered among the most serious categories of surveillance technology because they require no cooperation or error on the part of the target. The use of the digital advertising ecosystem as a delivery mechanism is a notable development, as it potentially allows surveillance tools to reach users through ordinary web browsing. Citizen Lab has previously documented the use of commercial spyware against journalists, activists, and government officials.
