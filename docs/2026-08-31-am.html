FDA Proposes Requiring Food Companies to Notify Agency Before Adding New Ingredients

The FDA has proposed a rule that would require food and beverage companies to notify the agency before adding new ingredients to products, closing a nearly 70-year gap in which companies could self-certify that ingredients were safe without informing regulators. For nearly 70 years, a provision in US food law has allowed manufacturers to add new ingredients to food and beverages without notifying the Food and Drug Administration, provided the company determined on its own that the ingredient was safe. The FDA has now proposed changing that framework to require companies to inform the agency when they introduce a new substance into human food. Under the existing system, the FDA frequently does not have a complete picture of what additives are in the food supply. The proposal would give the agency an opportunity to review new ingredients before they reach consumers, rather than relying entirely on the manufacturer's internal safety determination. The change would represent a significant shift in how food ingredient safety is regulated in the United States. The proposal is open to public comment before any final rule is issued.
