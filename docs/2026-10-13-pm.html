Trump Accounts Automatically Enrolled More Than 60 Million Children; New Rules Allow Donor Stock Contributions

The Treasury Department has automatically enrolled more than 60 million children in Trump Accounts, a savings program. New rules now allow donors to contribute individual stocks that families cannot choose, reject, or sell for five years. The Treasury Department has expanded a federally established child savings program, known as Trump Accounts, by automatically enrolling more than 60 million eligible children. The expansion includes new rules that change how the accounts operate: donors can now contribute individual stocks rather than only cash. Families receiving donated stocks cannot choose which stocks they receive, cannot reject them, and must hold them for a minimum of five years. Critics and analysts have raised questions about whether these constraints are appropriate for a government-administered savings program intended to benefit children. The mandatory holding period and lack of choice could expose account holders to concentrated risk if a donated stock declines in value. The program's scale — covering tens of millions of children — makes its design and rules consequential for a large share of American families.
