SpaceX Successfully Catches Starship Spacecraft on Return from Orbit in Texas

SpaceX's Starship spacecraft returned from orbit Saturday and was caught by mechanical arms at its Texas launch tower — a first in spaceflight history. The first-stage booster exploded shortly after launch Friday, preventing a planned double catch. SpaceX achieved a significant milestone in rocket reusability on Saturday when its Starship spacecraft, returning from a roughly 22-hour orbital mission, descended into the mechanical arms of its launch tower at the company's facility in Boca Chica, Texas. It was the first time a spacecraft of this scale had been caught on its return from space, using a system of large mechanical arms nicknamed 'chopsticks' that are attached to the launch tower. The 171-foot upper stage of the Starship vehicle completed approximately 14 orbits before making its return. The company had aimed to also catch the first-stage Super Heavy booster, but that booster exploded shortly after Friday's liftoff, leaving only the upper stage catch to be completed. Starship is the world's largest rocket at 407 feet tall when fully assembled. SpaceX is developing the vehicle as a fully reusable launch system, with the ability to catch and re-launch both stages central to that vision. The vehicle is also central to NASA's Artemis III program, which aims to return astronauts to the Moon, where Starship is planned to serve as the lunar landing vehicle. Reusing rockets by catching them rather than landing them on legs is intended to accelerate turnaround times and reduce costs, though the approach remains in active development.
