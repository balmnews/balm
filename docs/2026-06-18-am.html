US and Iran Sign Preliminary Agreement to End Conflict, Open Strait of Hormuz

President Trump and Iranian President Pezeshkian signed a memorandum of understanding aimed at ending the US-Iran war, opening the Strait of Hormuz, and beginning a 60-day negotiation period toward a final agreement. The United States and Iran signed a preliminary memorandum of understanding on Wednesday, with President Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian both affixing their signatures alongside Pakistan's prime minister. The agreement is designed to halt the ongoing conflict, reopen the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping, and set a 60-day clock for negotiations toward a comprehensive final deal. Key terms include Iran diluting its stockpile of highly enriched uranium and the lifting of US-backed sanctions on Iranian oil exports. Vice President Vance, who is expected to travel to Switzerland to lead the next phase of negotiations, said the process starts immediately and that the US is not providing direct financial transfers to Iran — though oil sanctions relief takes effect right away. Trump separately warned that the US would resume military action if Iran does not abide by the terms. The deal has drawn bipartisan skepticism in Congress, with lawmakers on both sides questioning whether Iran emerges from the agreement in a stronger position than before the conflict began. Analysts note that while Iran's nuclear infrastructure sustained damage during the war, its government survived and, in some assessments, has gained political leverage. The agreement does not constitute a final peace treaty, and significant details remain subject to the ongoing negotiations.

Finland's Parliament Votes to Lift Decades-Old Ban on Nuclear Weapons

Finland's parliament voted to remove a longstanding prohibition on nuclear weapons from the country's soil, a shift in defense policy the government described as aligning Finland more closely with NATO's deterrence posture. Finland's parliament approved legislation lifting a decades-old ban on nuclear weapons, marking a significant shift in the country's defense policy since joining NATO. Finnish officials framed the decision as necessary to bring the country's legal framework into alignment with NATO's collective deterrence doctrine, which relies on the possibility of deploying nuclear weapons across alliance territory. The move does not mean Finland will acquire nuclear weapons of its own, but removes the legal barrier to hosting allied nuclear assets or participating in NATO nuclear-sharing arrangements. Finland, which shares a lengthy border with Russia, joined NATO in 2023 following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The vote reflects the broader transformation in Nordic and Baltic security posture that has taken place since that invasion.

Supreme Court Unanimously Rules Federal Gun Ban for Drug Users Is Unconstitutional as Applied

The Supreme Court ruled unanimously that a federal law prohibiting illegal drug users from possessing firearms cannot be broadly applied to all drug users, finding it violated the Second Amendment in the case of a Texas man who used marijuana while keeping a firearm for self-defense. The Supreme Court issued a unanimous ruling on Wednesday limiting the reach of a federal law that makes it a crime for anyone who uses illegal drugs to possess a firearm. The case centered on Ali Danial Hemani, a Texas man who acknowledged regularly using marijuana but who was not charged with any other crime and was not accused of using the weapon while under the influence. The court found that applying the law to Hemani's circumstances violated his Second Amendment right to bear arms and that the statute was unconstitutionally vague as applied. The ruling does not strike down the federal drug-user gun prohibition entirely, but it significantly constrains how broadly prosecutors can apply it. The decision is also notable because the law in question was the same one used in the federal prosecution of Hunter Biden, the son of former President Joe Biden, on gun charges. Legal analysts noted the ruling could affect pending and future cases involving gun possession by drug users who were not accused of any related violent conduct. The 9-0 outcome marks the court's latest decision expanding the scope of Second Amendment protections.
