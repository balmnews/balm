Trump Administration Reinterprets Endangered Species Act to Exclude Incidental Harm

An internal administration memo reinterprets the Endangered Species Act so that only the intentional killing of protected animals is illegal, removing federal protections against incidental harm caused by development, industry, or other activities. The Trump administration has circulated an internal memo that reinterprets a core provision of the Endangered Species Act, narrowing the law's protections to apply only to the deliberate killing of listed species. Under the existing interpretation, the law has long been understood to prohibit activities that harm or harass protected animals as a side effect of otherwise lawful actions, such as land development, logging, or industrial operations. The new interpretation would strip that protection, meaning that businesses and developers could cause significant harm to endangered species populations without legal consequence as long as the harm was not intentional. Conservation and environmental law groups are expected to challenge the reinterpretation in court. The change does not require Congressional approval since it is framed as an interpretive shift rather than a statutory amendment, though courts will ultimately determine whether the new reading is consistent with the law's text and intent. The practical effect, if upheld, would be to significantly reduce the regulatory burden the Endangered Species Act places on development and industrial activity.
