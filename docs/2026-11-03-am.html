Americans Vote in 2026 Midterm Elections as Control of Congress Is Decided

Americans went to the polls Tuesday in the 2026 midterm elections, with control of both the House and Senate at stake along with 36 governorships and 145 ballot measures including abortion rights and voter ID laws across multiple states. Voters across the United States cast ballots Tuesday in the 2026 midterm elections, which will determine whether Republicans retain control of the House and Senate or Democrats gain a majority in one or both chambers. Control of the House requires reaching 218 seats, while the Senate requires 51 seats for Democrats or 50 for Republicans, given Vice President JD Vance's tiebreaking role. Thirty-six states are electing governors — representing approximately 80% of the national population — and Democrats are attempting to win a majority of governors' offices for the first time in two decades. Republicans currently hold 26 governorships. Voters are also deciding 145 proposed laws and constitutional amendments, including abortion rights measures in several states, congressional redistricting questions, and voter ID requirements. California voters are weighing Proposition 40, which would impose a one-time 5% tax on the wealth of residents with net worth above one billion dollars, with revenue directed toward replacing federal healthcare funding cuts. Alaska and Maine are using ranked choice voting in their Senate races, which may delay result timing in those states. Early voting was notably high heading into Election Day, with approximately 22% of Massachusetts voters having already cast ballots before polls opened. If Democrats gain control of Congress, Republican and Democratic officials both expect the result to trigger significant oversight activity, including investigations into the administration and potentially impeachment proceedings.

Health Insurers Increasingly Withhold Emergency Care Payments Despite Federal Law

Health insurers are frequently refusing to pay medical bills for emergency care, according to reporting, despite a federal law meant to end surprise billing — prompting disputes that have generated millions of dollars in arbitration proceedings. A federal law designed to eliminate surprise medical bills for patients has become the center of a growing payment dispute between health insurers and physicians, with insurers increasingly refusing to pay for emergency care costs they are legally required to cover. The No Surprises Act was enacted to protect patients from unexpected out-of-network charges, but according to reporting by the New York Times, health plans are now routinely withholding payments from doctors and healthcare facilities, forcing disputes into a high-dollar arbitration system that has generated millions in contested payouts. Physicians argue that insurers are flouting consumer protection law. Insurers contend that the arbitration process has been gamed by healthcare providers seeking inflated payments. The dispute has direct implications for patients, who may face delayed care or find fewer providers willing to accept their insurance, and for the long-term functioning of the emergency care system. The issue is drawing attention from regulators and consumer advocates.

Affordable Care Act Premiums Rising 13% in 2027 for Unsubsidized Buyers

The lowest-cost bronze plan under the Affordable Care Act will cost 13% more in 2027 for people purchasing coverage without a subsidy, with the largest increases affecting older adults. Monthly premiums for the lowest-cost bronze plan available under the Affordable Care Act will increase by 13% in 2027 for people who purchase health insurance without a federal subsidy, according to data analyzed by the New York Times. The increase is not uniform — older adults face the steepest premium hikes. Americans who receive income-based subsidies through the ACA marketplace may be partially shielded from the full increase, as their subsidies are tied to benchmark plan costs. However, those who earn above subsidy thresholds will absorb the full rise. Healthcare affordability has been a persistent concern for self-employed individuals, small business owners, and workers whose employers do not offer coverage. The 13% increase comes at a time when household budgets remain under pressure from broader inflation and elevated costs in other essential categories.

Binance Reports $6.3 Billion Hack Carried Out via Fake Job Interviews

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance disclosed that hackers stole $6.3 billion from customer accounts over a months-long campaign that began with fake job interviews targeting one of its engineers, who had malware installed on a work computer. Cryptocurrency exchange Binance has released a detailed account of a $6.3 billion hack that, if confirmed at that scale, would rank among the largest financial thefts ever recorded. According to Binance's report, the attack was a months-long operation in which an engineer was contacted through a legitimate job-hunting platform and invited to participate in fake job interviews with fraudulent company representatives. During that process, malicious software was installed on the engineer's work computer, eventually giving the attackers access to an internal artificial intelligence tool. That access was used to gain control of the company's cryptocurrency wallets, from which the funds were taken. Binance has been working to address the breach and limit reputational damage. The incident highlights a growing pattern of so-called social engineering attacks targeting employees at financial technology firms — where the initial compromise is human rather than purely technical. For American users of cryptocurrency exchanges and for investors in the broader digital asset market, the breach raises questions about the security of centralized crypto platforms and the recourse available when funds are stolen.
