The federal government has announced reductions to Colorado River water allocations for three states in response to continuing drought conditions that have drawn down reservoirs to historically low levels. Arizona faces the largest proportional cut, with its share of the river set to shrink by approximately one-third. Officials expect the reduction will increase Arizona's reliance on groundwater, which is already under pressure from population growth and agricultural demand. The Colorado River supplies water to roughly 40 million people across seven US states and parts of Mexico, supporting cities, farms, and tribal lands across the American West. Reservoir levels at Lake Mead and Lake Powell, the two largest storage facilities on the river, have declined significantly in recent years due to a combination of prolonged drought, increased demand, and the effects of climate change on snowpack in the Rocky Mountains. The cuts are part of ongoing negotiations among the seven basin states and the federal government over how to manage the river as the gap between supply and demand widens.
