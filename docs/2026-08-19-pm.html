US National Debt Reaches $40 Trillion for the First Time

The US national debt has exceeded $40 trillion for the first time, reaching $40.047 trillion. Rising yields on government bonds are pushing up borrowing costs across the economy. The US national debt reached $40.047 trillion this week, surpassing the $40 trillion threshold for the first time in the country's history. The milestone reflects a decade in which the federal deficit roughly doubled, driven by a combination of military spending, tax cuts, and tariff-related refunds under the current administration. The rapid pace of debt accumulation has unsettled bond markets: yields on 10-year, 20-year, and 30-year Treasury notes all reached 20-year highs this week, with the 30-year yield particularly elevated. As investors demand higher returns to hold US government debt, those increased rates ripple through the broader economy, raising borrowing costs for mortgages, auto loans, student loans, and business financing. The US Treasury announced it is doubling its buyback of government debt in an effort to stabilize the bond market. Federal Reserve officials, per minutes from the July meeting, are divided on the path forward, with many indicating they would support raising the central bank's key interest rate further if inflation does not subside. The combination of record debt, rising yields, and Fed uncertainty represents a significant economic crosscurrent heading into the fall.

Astronomers Identify Fastest Known Star in the Milky Way Orbiting Central Black Hole

Astronomers have detected a star designated S301 traveling at approximately 25,000 kilometers per second around the supermassive black hole at the center of the Milky Way, making it the fastest known star in the galaxy. A team of astronomers has identified the fastest known star in the Milky Way, a body called S301 that orbits the supermassive black hole at the galaxy's center at roughly 25,000 kilometers — about 15,500 miles — per second. That speed is approximately 100,000 times faster than a commercial aircraft. S301 is also the closest known star to the galactic center's black hole, known as Sagittarius A-star. Researchers say the star's extreme proximity and velocity make it a valuable natural laboratory for studying the physics of supermassive black holes and the conditions near them. The discovery was made using telescope observations and reported in new scientific findings published this week.
