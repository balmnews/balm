US and Iran Sign Initial Agreement to End War and Reopen Strait of Hormuz

The United States and Iran have signed a preliminary memorandum of understanding to end their conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz to shipping, with the strait expected to be open by Friday. Key terms have not been made public, and significant issues remain for further negotiation. The United States and Iran signed an initial memorandum of understanding on Monday aimed at ending their war and reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the critical waterway through which a substantial share of the world's oil supply passes. President Trump declared the deal already signed and said the strait would be fully open by Friday, though the specific terms of the agreement have not been publicly released, and both sides acknowledged that major issues remain to be resolved in follow-on negotiations over a 60-day period. The conflict, which lasted more than 100 days, caused what analysts described as the most significant disruption to global energy supplies on record. However, energy analysts cautioned that oil and gas prices are unlikely to return to pre-conflict levels for months, even as the strait reopens, because global supply chains and shipping logistics will take time to normalize. The deal's durability faces significant uncertainty. Israel's government expressed reservations, with Prime Minister Netanyahu declaring victory over Iran while simultaneously declining to endorse or fully align with the agreement's terms. Israel's defense minister stated that Israeli forces would remain in Lebanon indefinitely. The Israeli military continued strikes in the region during the signing period, complicating the diplomatic picture. Some analysts characterized the deal as a strategic setback for Israel, while Iranian state media framed it as a victory over both the United States and Israel. The Trump administration stated it would maintain approximately 50,000 troops in the Middle East during the two-month negotiation window. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy also used the G7 gathering in Évian-les-Bains, France — where the deal was center stage — to press for a meeting with Putin and advance Ukraine peace talks. Prime Minister Keir Starmer announced Monday that the United Kingdom will prohibit children under 16 from accessing major social media platforms, with the ban expected to take effect by spring 2027. Critics also raised questions about the technical feasibility of robust age verification at scale. Supporters of the measure argue that unrestricted social media access poses documented risks to children's mental health and safety. The age verification framework and its enforcement mechanisms are still being developed.

A new report found that state laws prohibiting PFAS — a class of synthetic chemicals sometimes called forever chemicals due to their persistence in the environment and human body — in clothing and textiles have substantially reduced their presence in consumer products.
