Germany's AfD Wins Saxony-Anhalt State Election with 44% of the Vote, Its Best Result Since WWII

Germany's far-right Alternative for Germany party won the Saxony-Anhalt state election with roughly 44% of the vote, its strongest result in any German election since the Nazi era, falling just short of an outright majority needed to govern alone. The Alternative for Germany party, known as the AfD, won the state election in Saxony-Anhalt on Sunday with approximately 44% of the vote — a result that represents the strongest performance by a far-right party in a German state election since the end of World War Two. The party fell about three seats short of an outright majority in the state legislature, meaning it cannot govern without coalition partners. Mainstream German parties have long maintained a so-called firewall, a collective refusal to enter into governing coalitions with the AfD, and that policy now faces its most significant test. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called the result a grave electoral defeat and said the government could not simply move past it. The AfD's lead candidate in Saxony-Anhalt, Ulrich Siegmund, credited support from figures including Elon Musk, who had publicly backed the party. Russian state media and European nationalist politicians also celebrated the result. Saxony-Anhalt is a state in the former communist eastern Germany, a region where the AfD has consistently polled higher than in the west. The party ran primarily on anti-immigration and anti-European Union positions and has been described by domestic intelligence authorities as a suspected extremist organization in parts of its structure. Several more regional elections are scheduled across Germany in the coming months, and the Saxony-Anhalt result is likely to intensify pressure on mainstream parties to respond to voter concerns driving AfD support, particularly on immigration and economic anxiety.

UN Report Finds Wildfires and Heatwaves Are Reversing Decades of Air Quality Progress

A new UN weather agency report finds that rising wildfire activity and extreme heat are offsetting air quality gains achieved through cleaner industrial and vehicle emissions, with serious health consequences worldwide. A report from the World Meteorological Organization has found that increasing wildfires and heatwaves are eroding decades of progress in reducing air pollution. Regions that had recorded meaningful improvements in air quality — achieved through cleaner vehicle standards and reduced industrial emissions — are now experiencing new threats as smoke from large-scale wildfires adds pollutants back into the atmosphere. The report describes this as a feedback loop: climate-driven extreme weather worsens air quality, which in turn has serious health consequences for populations. The findings come as cities around the world grapple with record-breaking summer temperatures. Urban policymakers in multiple countries have begun treating heat and air quality as interconnected public health challenges rather than separate environmental concerns.
