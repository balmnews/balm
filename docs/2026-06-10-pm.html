The acceleration is attributed primarily to a sharp rise in gasoline prices following the start of the U.S.-Iran conflict and the disruption of oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz. Inflation stood at 3.3% in March and has climbed steadily since. The May figure has put investors on edge, with markets weighing the possibility that the Federal Reserve may respond by raising interest rates to restrain prices — a move that would increase borrowing costs for consumers and businesses. The sustained rise in energy costs has rippled through a broad range of consumer goods. The combination of military conflict in the Middle East and constrained global oil supply has produced economic conditions that analysts describe as a significant pressure on American households. How long those pressures persist depends in part on the trajectory of the conflict and whether oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz are restored.
