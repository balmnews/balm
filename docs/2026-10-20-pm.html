UK Approves First New North Sea Gas Field Since 2024 Moratorium

The UK government has approved the Jackdaw gasfield in the North Sea, located roughly 150 miles east of Aberdeen, making it the first new gas extraction project approved since a government moratorium was introduced in 2024. The British government announced approval of the Jackdaw natural gas field, situated approximately 150 miles east of Aberdeen, Scotland. The decision marks the first new North Sea gas extraction project to receive consent since the government imposed a moratorium on new drilling in 2024. The approval has been anticipated for roughly four years and has changed hands across six energy secretaries. The announcement was made by Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh and Chancellor John Healey. Proponents argue the field supports domestic energy production and energy security. Critics, including climate advocates and a number of Labour MPs, contend the decision undermines the government's environmental commitments. The move signals Prime Minister Andy Burnham's government is prioritizing energy security and the North Sea fossil fuel sector alongside its stated climate goals. Sources differ on the extent to which the field will materially reduce household energy bills; most analysts suggest any impact would be modest and delayed. The decision is expected to intensify internal debate within the Labour Party over climate policy ahead of the COP31 summit.

Idaho OB-GYNs Describe Deteriorating Conditions for Women's Health Since Abortion Ban

A reported investigation describes obstetric physicians in Idaho facing severe professional and legal pressure following the state's abortion ban, with many leaving the state and those remaining describing increasingly difficult conditions for maternal care. An investigation found that Idaho's near-total abortion ban has been accompanied by a significant departure of obstetricians and gynecologists from the state, as physicians describe uncertainty over what procedures are legally permissible and fear of prosecution. Doctors who remained described managing high-risk pregnancies, miscarriages, and other obstetric emergencies under conditions they characterized as increasingly dangerous for patients. The report details cases in which physicians said legal ambiguity delayed or complicated standard medical interventions. Idaho has one of the strictest abortion laws in the country, with narrow exceptions that physicians say are difficult to apply in real clinical situations. The state's rural geography means that the departure of specialists in one area can leave large populations without access to obstetric care. The situation in Idaho is being watched closely by health policy researchers as a potential indicator of how strict abortion bans affect maternal health infrastructure more broadly.
