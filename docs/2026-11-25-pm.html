Global Temperatures Exceed 2°C Above Preindustrial Levels for Six Consecutive Days

For the first time on record, global average surface temperatures exceeded 2 degrees Celsius above preindustrial levels for six consecutive days, with the streak forecast to continue. Scientists say the event does not by itself confirm a permanent breach of the Paris Agreement threshold, but describes it as a significant marker. The world has experienced its first recorded streak of six consecutive days with average global surface temperatures more than 2 degrees Celsius above preindustrial levels, with daily readings reaching as high as 2.24°C above baseline on Monday. Climate scientists emphasize that a brief streak of this kind does not by itself mean the upper limit of the Paris Agreement's temperature target has been permanently breached — that determination requires sustained temperature averages over decades, not days. However, researchers describe the event as a significant and concerning marker of how frequently extreme temperature conditions are occurring. The streak was still forecast to continue at the time of reporting. The 2°C threshold set in the Paris Agreement represents the level beyond which scientists warn of substantially more severe climate impacts, including more intense heat waves, flooding, droughts, and sea level rise.
