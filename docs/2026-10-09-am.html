Margaret Hamilton, Pioneer of Software Engineering and Apollo 11, Dies at 90

Margaret Hamilton, the computer scientist whose software guided the Apollo 11 moon landing, has died at age 90. MIT announced her death; she passed away on September 30. Margaret Hamilton, widely credited as a foundational figure in the development of software engineering, died on September 30 at age 90. The Massachusetts Institute of Technology announced her passing. Hamilton led the team at MIT that developed the onboard flight software for NASA's Apollo program, including the code that guided Apollo 11 to the first crewed moon landing in 1969. During the descent to the lunar surface, her error-detection and recovery software allowed the mission to continue despite a series of computer alarms — a moment widely regarded as one of the most consequential software decisions in history. Working at a time when software development had no established professional framework, Hamilton is credited with coining the term software engineering and arguing for its recognition as a rigorous discipline. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016. Her death marks the loss of one of the central figures of the early computing era.
