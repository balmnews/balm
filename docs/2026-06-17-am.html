Federal Reserve Holds Rates at Kevin Warsh's First Meeting as Chairman

The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady at Kevin Warsh's first policy meeting as chairman, with markets and analysts watching closely for signals about the new leadership's direction on rates and the central bank's balance sheet. The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate unchanged at its June meeting, the first presided over by Kevin Warsh, who was nominated by President Trump to lead the central bank. Warsh held a news conference following the decision, marking his public debut as chairman. Markets had widely anticipated no rate change, but were focused on any signals Warsh might offer about his longer-term approach to monetary policy. Warsh has previously expressed particular interest in reducing the Fed's balance sheet, which expanded significantly during the pandemic era through bond-buying programs. The Fed's balance sheet currently holds trillions in assets, and any shift in how aggressively it is wound down could affect mortgage rates, Treasury yields, and broader financial conditions. The political dimension of Warsh's appointment is significant: Trump nominated him with the expectation of a more accommodating approach to interest rates, though the Fed chair is legally independent in setting policy. The meeting also included the release of the Fed's summary of economic projections, which provides forecasts for growth, inflation, and the path of interest rates. Warsh's first meeting drew substantial attention from investors, economists, and lawmakers as a signal of whether the institution's approach to inflation and employment will shift under new leadership.
