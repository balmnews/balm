Supreme Court Strikes Down Trump Order Ending Birthright Citizenship

The Supreme Court ruled that children born in the United States to parents who are in the country without legal status are entitled to citizenship, striking down an executive order seeking to end birthright citizenship. On the final day of its 2025–26 term, the Supreme Court struck down President Trump's executive order that sought to end birthright citizenship, affirming that the 14th Amendment guarantees citizenship to nearly all children born on U.S. soil. The ruling is a significant setback for the administration's immigration agenda. The court's written opinion stated, "We break no new ground today," signaling that the justices viewed the constitutional question as settled rather than novel. Birthright citizenship — the principle that anyone born on U.S. soil is automatically a citizen regardless of their parents' immigration status — has been in place since the 14th Amendment was ratified in 1868. Trump's order, issued early in his second term, directed federal agencies to stop recognizing citizenship for children born to parents who are in the country without legal authorization or on temporary visas. Multiple federal courts had blocked the order, and the Supreme Court's ruling resolves the legal challenge with finality. Legal scholars and civil rights groups welcomed the decision. The ruling does not resolve all aspects of the administration's immigration enforcement agenda, but it forecloses one of its most far-reaching proposals. The court also issued rulings on transgender athletes and campaign finance on the same day, closing out a consequential term.
