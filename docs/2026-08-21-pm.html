Federal Government Mandates Colorado River Water Cuts of Up to 40% for Arizona, California, and Nevada

The US government has announced mandatory water allocation cuts of up to 40% for three states drawing from the Colorado River, as a long-term drought continues to strain the river's supply. The federal government has announced mandatory reductions in water allocations from the Colorado River for Arizona, California, and Nevada as part of a ten-year framework intended to address a prolonged drought and the depletion of the river's reservoirs. Arizona faces the steepest cuts, with its allocation set to shrink by roughly a third, which is expected to increase dependence on groundwater and intensify pressure on the agricultural sector. California and Nevada also face reductions under the plan. The Colorado River is the primary water source for approximately 40 million people across seven US states and parts of Mexico, supplying water for cities, farms, and industry across the American West. The river's two main reservoirs, Lake Mead and Lake Powell, have dropped significantly over the past two decades due to a combination of overallocation, rising temperatures, and reduced snowpack. The previous governing framework for the river expired and negotiations over a replacement have been prolonged and contentious. Experts have noted that while the new plan represents a significant step, the mandated cuts may still be insufficient to fully stabilize the river system given ongoing climate trends. Agriculture, which accounts for the majority of Colorado River water use, is expected to bear much of the impact from reduced allocations.

Moderna and Merck Report mRNA Vaccine Prevented Melanoma Recurrence in High-Risk Patients

Moderna and Merck say their experimental mRNA vaccine prevented the return and spread of melanoma in high-risk patients, a significant development in cancer treatment research. Moderna and Merck have announced that their experimental mRNA-based cancer vaccine prevented the recurrence and spread of melanoma in high-risk patients in a clinical trial. Melanoma is the most dangerous form of skin cancer, and patients who have had high-risk tumors removed face a significant chance of the cancer returning or spreading. The vaccine is personalized — designed for each individual based on the specific mutations in their tumor — and represents an application of the mRNA technology platform developed for Covid-19 vaccines. The companies say the trial results demonstrate a meaningful reduction in recurrence among patients who received the vaccine compared to those who did not. The findings build on earlier-stage results that generated significant scientific interest. The development contributed to a substantial rise in Moderna's stock price. The vaccine has not yet received regulatory approval and would need to complete the full approval process before becoming available to patients. If approved, it would represent the first mRNA cancer vaccine to reach the market.

The broader cattle shortage reflects the combined effect of years of drought affecting grazing land, rising feed costs, and herd liquidation decisions made by ranchers over the past several years. Rebuilding cattle herds takes years, meaning elevated beef prices are expected to persist regardless of near-term import policy changes.

Developing El Niño Expected to Be Strongest in Over a Century, Threatening Extreme Weather Globally

The UK's Met Office forecasts that a developing El Niño event over the Pacific Ocean is likely to be the strongest in more than a century, with 2027 projected to break heat records and severe weather impacts expected worldwide. Scientists at the UK's Met Office and other research institutions are warning that a developing El Niño weather pattern in the Pacific Ocean is on track to be the most powerful in over a century. El Niño is a natural climate phenomenon characterized by unusually warm Pacific Ocean surface temperatures that can shift weather patterns globally, typically intensifying drought in some regions and increasing rainfall and storms in others. The Met Office describes the developing event as unprecedented in its projected strength. For the United States, strong El Niño events historically correlate with wetter winters across the southern tier of the country, drier conditions in the Pacific Northwest, and altered hurricane seasons. Globally, the phenomenon is expected to drive severe droughts in some regions, increased flooding in others, and could make 2027 the hottest year on record by a significant margin, compounding the effects of background warming from climate change. In the UK, forecasters expect increased autumn rainfall and storm activity. Scientists note that the event arrives against a backdrop of already elevated global temperatures, which is expected to amplify its effects beyond what historical comparisons alone would suggest. Infrastructure sectors including transportation are already beginning to plan for more extreme heat conditions in the coming year.
