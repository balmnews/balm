House Votes Unanimously to Eliminate the Penny

The House of Representatives passed the Common Cents Act unanimously, ending the minting of the one-cent coin and establishing a system of rounding cash transactions to the nearest five cents. The bill moves to the Senate. The House of Representatives voted unanimously to pass the Common Cents Act, which would permanently end production of the one-cent coin. Under the legislation, cash transactions would be rounded to the nearest five cents at the point of sale. Electronic and card transactions would not be affected. Supporters of the measure have argued that the penny costs more to produce than its face value and serves little practical economic purpose. The bill now moves to the Senate for consideration.

Over 1.2 Million Children Have Lost SNAP Benefits Since July 2025

More than 1.2 million children have been removed from the federal Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program since July 2025, according to data reviewed by PBS NewsHour. Experts say the reduction does not reflect a corresponding drop in food insecurity. More than 1.2 million children have lost access to SNAP benefits — the federal food assistance program formerly known as food stamps — since July of last year. Experts quoted in coverage of the data said the decline is unlikely to reflect an actual reduction in need, and expressed concern that the losses are leaving food-insecure families without adequate support. SNAP is the primary federal nutrition safety net for low-income households with children. Policy changes to eligibility rules, administrative requirements, and enrollment processes have been cited as likely contributing factors. The scale of the reduction has drawn attention from nutrition policy researchers and advocates.

Houthi rebels have seized Perim Island in the Bab al-Mandab Strait and now control Yemen's western coast, a strategic chokepoint for global shipping. Yemen's Houthi rebels have captured Perim Island and now control the western coast of Yemen, including territory adjacent to the Bab al-Mandab Strait — a narrow waterway through which a substantial share of global oil and commercial shipping passes. The Strait is sometimes referred to as a critical chokepoint because its closure or disruption would significantly affect tanker routes between the Middle East and European markets. Separately, a major Saudi Arabian pipeline was reported to be out of service for several weeks following an attack, compounding pressure on oil supply and prices. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman met with US Central Command leadership, reportedly requesting additional US military assistance after the Houthi offensive escalated. Analysts have noted that the Houthi financial network, which enables the group to sustain its military operations despite US sanctions, spans Iranian oil revenues, cryptocurrency channels, and other sources. The combination of Red Sea shipping pressure and the Saudi pipeline disruption has contributed to rising oil prices and related concerns about diesel costs in the United States and globally.
