Large Study Finds Retatrutide Reduces Body Weight by Up to 25% and Improves Multiple Health Markers

The weight-loss drug retatrutide, which mimics three gut hormones that regulate appetite and metabolism, reduced body weight by roughly a quarter in the largest study of its kind and also improved blood sugar and reduced inflammation. A major clinical study of retatrutide found that the drug reduced participants' body weight by approximately 25% on average, making it among the most effective weight-loss medications reported to date. Unlike existing drugs in the GLP-1 class such as semaglutide, retatrutide simultaneously targets three gut hormones — GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon — that regulate appetite, blood sugar, and metabolism. Beyond weight reduction, the study found improvements in blood sugar levels and markers of inflammation, suggesting potential benefits for conditions including type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease. The drug is not yet approved by regulatory authorities, but its results are drawing significant attention from researchers and the pharmaceutical industry. The findings suggest a new category of treatment that could broaden the already rapidly growing field of obesity medicine. Larger and longer-term studies will be needed to assess safety and durability of results before regulatory review.
