California health officials are warning residents of potential measles exposure across six counties after an infected and unvaccinated individual traveled through the state using Amtrak trains and thruway bus connections. Officials are urging anyone who may have been on the same routes during the relevant time periods to check their vaccination status and watch for symptoms. Measles is highly contagious and spreads through the air; a single infected person can transmit the virus to up to 90 percent of unvaccinated people they encounter. The warning comes as the United States is experiencing its largest measles resurgence in more than three decades, driven largely by declining vaccination rates in some communities. Symptoms typically appear seven to fourteen days after exposure and include fever, cough, runny nose, red eyes, and a characteristic rash. The MMR vaccine is highly effective at preventing infection. Study Finds US Military Veterans Have Higher Levels of PFAS 'Forever Chemicals' Than Civilians

A peer-reviewed study found that US military veterans carry significantly higher concentrations of PFAS 'forever chemicals' in their blood than the general civilian population, linked to elevated PFAS contamination around military bases from firefighting foam. A new peer-reviewed study has found that US military veterans have substantially higher levels of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — known as PFAS, or 'forever chemicals' — in their blood compared to civilians. The elevated exposure is linked to PFAS-containing firefighting foams historically used at military installations, which have contaminated soil and water around bases across the country. PFAS compounds are called 'forever chemicals' because they do not break down naturally in the environment or the human body and have been associated with a range of health concerns including certain cancers, immune system effects, and thyroid disruption. The findings raise additional questions about the long-term health consequences for veterans who served at or near affected installations, and about the adequacy of existing medical monitoring and compensation programs for PFAS-exposed service members. Efforts to clean up PFAS-contaminated military sites have been ongoing, though progress has been slow and costs are substantial.

Fiji Declares National HIV Emergency as Cases Surge

Fiji has declared a national emergency over a dramatic rise in HIV infections, with an estimated one in 60 adults now living with the virus. Authorities link the surge to increased drug use and needle sharing. Fiji has declared a national public health emergency in response to a sharp increase in HIV infections. Approximately one in 60 adults in the Pacific island nation is now estimated to be living with the virus. Health authorities recorded around 2,000 new cases in 2025 alone — a dramatic rise for a country of Fiji's size. Officials attribute the surge primarily to growing intravenous drug use and the sharing of needles, which enables rapid transmission. Fiji is a popular international travel and tourism destination, including for American visitors. The government's emergency declaration is intended to unlock additional resources and accelerate the public health response, including expanded testing, treatment access, and harm reduction programs. HIV is manageable with antiretroviral treatment, but access to consistent medical care is a challenge in many Pacific island nations. The declaration signals that existing health infrastructure has been insufficient to contain the spread.
