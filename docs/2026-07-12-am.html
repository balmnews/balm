Trump Administration Removes Key Endangered Species Act Habitat Protection

The Trump administration finalized a rule eliminating a 50-year-old Endangered Species Act provision that had prohibited activities harming the habitats of protected wildlife, opening those areas to logging, mining, and other development. The Trump administration has finalized a regulatory change that removes a foundational protection under the Endangered Species Act that had been in place for approximately 50 years. The rule had prohibited activities that harm or harass the habitats of imperiled species — a protection that conservationists say has been central to preventing extinctions, with the law credited with keeping roughly 99 percent of listed species from going extinct. Under the new rule, habitats of endangered and threatened species can be opened to logging, mining, drilling, and other development activities. Environmental groups and scientists have raised concerns that habitat destruction is the leading driver of species loss, and that removing this protection could accelerate declines among already vulnerable populations. The administration has framed the change as reducing regulatory burdens. The rule change does not require congressional approval and takes effect as a final agency action. Conservation organizations have indicated legal challenges are likely.
